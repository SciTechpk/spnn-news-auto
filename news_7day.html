Detailed analysis of the data showed that at least 17 children have lost their lives to measles — a highly contagious viral infection — at IH with around 850 cases this year so far. In addition, a child died of diphtheria at the hospital, which has recorded at least eight of cases of the bacterial infection. At SID&RC, at least 54 children — 36 from measles and 18 from diphtheria — have died during the period between Jan 1 and July 26, 2026. The total number of measles and diphtheria cases reported at the facility was 575 and 81, respectively. While detailed data of the NICH was not available, sources at the province’s largest public-sector hospital for children shared that at least seven children have died of measles at the facility in recent weeks. “We get complicated cases after the patient has already been mismanaged at multiple health facilities. He pointed out that the first shot of measles under the EPI programme was given at nine months and the second (the last) one at 15 months. “If 95 per cent of our children get the two shots at the right time, we can easily control this disease as the vaccine provides herd immunity to the rest of the population. Most children in our country, however, either fail to receive any dose or get only partial protection through one dose.” To address this challenge, he said countrywide vaccination drives were launched every four years. Last year, a national level drive was launched for children aged six months to five years, who were given an additional dose of MR (measles and rubella) vaccine. “Along with the government efforts, our healthcare providers have a crucial role to help tackle the vaccination coverage gap. When an ailing child reports to them, they must ask about his or her vaccination history/status and provide proper counselling to parents, if they find any vaccine doses missing. “Having said that, parents have the prime responsibility to protect their child and they must play their part with diligence. Pakistan gets a pass on new US visa bond rule

• Washington makes visa bond programme permanent; Bangladesh, Nepal among 50 countries covered, India also excluded• Visitors from listed nations face refundable deposits of up to $20,000 WASHINGTON: Pakistan is not among the 50 countries whose citizens may now be required to deposit up to $20,000 as a financial guarantee before obtaining a US visitor visa under a new programme that took effect on Aug 1. The list published in the US Federal Register does not include Pakistan. Among South Asian countries, Bangladesh and Nepal are covered by the programme, while India is not. Afghanistan and Iran are not included because the United States does not maintain normal diplomatic relations with either country. The State Department said the decision followed a review of the 2025 pilot programme, which it said significantly reduced visa overstays among travellers from participating countries and improved compliance with US immigration rules. Officials said those findings prompted Washington to make the programme permanent. According to the department, there were 45,488 overstays from these 50 countries in 2024. In the first 10 months of the pilot, the number of overstays was fewer than 50. The visa bond requirement has also resulted in a decrease in visa issuance, as some applicants appear to self-select by not paying a bond. Diplomatic sources also confirmed to Dawn that “Pakistan is not on the list of countries covered by the programme”. The programme applies to applicants for B-1 business and B-2 tourist visas from countries covered by the scheme. Under the new rules, US consular officers may require selected applicants to post a refundable bond of between $10,000 and $20,000 before issuing a visa. The regulations make permanent a visa bond pilot launched in 2025 after the US State Department concluded that it had helped reduce visa overstays. The new rules also increase the maximum bond from $15,000 to $20,000 and eliminate the previous $5,000 minimum bond available during the pilot programme. The 50 countries covered by the programme include 30 African nations as well as countries in Asia, the Caribbean and the Pacific. The bond will be refunded if the visitor complies with all visa conditions and leaves the United States before the authorised period of stay expires. Applicants who overstay or otherwise violate the terms of their visas risk forfeiting the deposit. The bond requirement does not apply automatically to all applicants from the countries covered by the programme. Govt executes agreements with pension fund managers

• 16 companies to establish separate pension funds, ensure death and disability risk cover for employees• Centre to establish non-banking finance company to assist in implementation, monitoring of pension scheme ISLAMABAD: The government has executed agreements with 16 pension fund managers to formally operationalise the Defined Contribution Pension Fund Scheme (DCPFS) launched a year ago for public sector employees as part of major pension reforms aimed at containing the snowballing pension burden on the budget. In an announcement, the Ministry of Finance said it had executed agreements with 16 eligible pension fund managers, most of them banks and insurance companies. Around 10 of these eligible fund managers are owned or led by major commercial banks in the country. They include ABL Asset Management Company, Al Habib Asset Management, Al Meezan Investment, Faysal Asset Management, JS Investments, MCB Investment, Alfalah Asset Management, HBL Asset Management, UBL Fund Managers and NBP Fund Management, The pension fund managers led by or owned by insurance companies include EFU Life Assurance and Pak-Qatar Family Takaful. The remaining funds — Atlas Asset Man­agement, Lucky Invest­ments, National Investments and AWT Investments are owned by various business groups. Under the agreements and rules, these fund managers would set up, operate and manage conventional or Shariah-compliant funds for state employees under the federal government-defined contributory pension fund scheme in accordance with the Voluntary Pension System Rules 2005. Only these eligible pension fund managers shall be authorised to launch and manage employer pension funds for which the government will ensure annual budgetary allocation for the employer’s contribution. The agreements entail standard terms and conditions for providing systems support and electronic transfer of contributions to the pension fund’s designated bank accounts and further allocation of units to the relevant sub-funds in each employee’s pension account as per the allocation policy. The agreements also envisage a mandatory insurance plan, providing death and disability risk cover to the employees, to be arranged by these fund managers. The eligible pension fund managers will establish separate pension funds, while the government is responsible for the creation of a Non-Banking Finance Company (NBFC) to assist in implementation and monitoring of the pension fund scheme. Till such time the NBFC is established and fully operationalised, the government itself, through the finance ministry, will perform the NBFC role and establish an online portal to facilitate opening of pension accounts of employees, obtain information and data from eligible pension fund managers or otherwise communicate with the fund managers. Under the rules, employees will not withdraw any amount from the pension account before retirement is notified. Even upon reaching retirement age, the employee shall not withdraw more than 25pc of the accumulated balance in the pension account, and shall invest the remaining amount in the manner prescribed in the Voluntary Pension System Rules, 2005, for a period of at least twenty years or till his death, whichever is earlier. However, the rules allow employees to transfer pension account balance from one eligible pension fund manager to another. The government had announced pension reforms in 2024 to introduce a contributory pension fund for all government employees, including civil government and armed forces personnel. In a formal announcement in the National Assembly, the government said the contributory pension scheme would apply to all civil employees of the federal government, including civilian employees paid from defence estimates appointed on a regular basis on or after July 1, 2024. A September 2024 notification stated that the scheme would also apply to armed forces personnel appointed on or after July 1, 2025. While the civilian side of the scheme has been implemented and the government has already set aside about Rs25bn for the scheme as its initial share, the contributory pension scheme for armed forces was deferred for a year to 2026 due to concerns over their peculiar nature of duties requiring detailed review, according to earlier statements by the finance minister and secretary. Officials said the contributory pension scheme for armed forces personnel, which had been postponed two years ago, had been further delayed owing to internal departmental consultations and challenging security conditions, hindering any move that could be seen demoralising personnel. The finance ministry notified in October 2025 that federal employees would contribute 10pc of their pensionable pay to qualify for a 12pc contribution from the public exchequer under the newly formalised contributory pension fund scheme. The total contribution rate will thus be 22pc, replacing the traditional pension system for new entrants. The October notification and pension rules superseded an earlier order of August 2024, which had set the government’s contribution at 20pc. On Aug 20, 2024, the ministry initially announced the introduction of the contributory pension scheme for new entrants to the civil government and armed forces. At that time, employees were to contribute 10pc of their basic pay, while the government was to contribute 20pc. The contributory pension scheme was introduced on the advice of international lenders, notably the World Bank, to contain the rising pension liabilities, which the government had itself described as a growing fiscal risk. It does not apply to existing employees but aims to slow future growth in pension liabilities. Federal pension expenditure is estimated at Rs1.170tr for FY2026-27, including about Rs860bn for military pensions and about Rs300bn for civil employees. Cotton production jumps 32 per cent

Textile mills have become reliant on imports amid falling cotton output.—PPI/file LAHORE: Phutti (seed-cotton) arrivals at ginning factories rose by more than 32 per cent year-on-year by July 31, driven by a sharp increase in Sindh despite a slight decline in Punjab, according to the figures released by the Pakistan Cotton Ginners Association (PCGA) on Monday. The PCGA fortnightly report revealed that phutti arrivals reached 785,822 bales by July 31, up 32.33pc from 593,821 bales received during the corresponding period last year. It showed that Punjab’s arrivals stood at 298,150 bales, down 1.1 per cent from a year earlier, while Sindh recorded 487,672 bales, marking a record 66.82pc increase despite recent monsoon rains. Balochistan also posted a 52.67pc rise in arrivals to 22,900 bales. Cotton Ginners Forum Chairman Ihsanul Haq pointed out a significant discrepancy between the PCGA figures and the Punjab government’s Crop Reporting Service (CRS). While the PCGA data showed phutti arrivals in Punjab at around 298,000 bales, the CRS claimed production of about 435,000 bales by July 30, roughly 32pc higher than the PCGA estimate. He said the difference warranted clarification from the relevant authorities. Sindh’s record 67pc surge by July 31 offsets Punjab decline despite rains He said the CRS figures also reflected an alarming collapse in cotton cultivation across Bahawalpur Division, where only around 17,000 bales were produced despite the region once being Pakistan’s leading cotton-growing belt. According to him, the rapid expansion of sugarcane cultivation, driven by the concentration of sugar mills in the region, has transformed the traditional cotton zone into a sugarcane belt, undermining domestic cotton production. Mr Haq warned that shrinking cotton acreage, due to increasing sugarcane sowing, was forcing the country to rely increasingly on imports of both cotton and edible oil. He urged the federal and provincial governments to restore cotton cultivation by restricting sugarcane farming in designated cotton-growing areas. Pakistan imported a record 3.482 million tonnes of edible oil worth $3.785 billion in 2025-26, compared with 3.214m tonnes valued at $3.40bn a year earlier. The PCGA report showed that textile mills purchased 704,000 bales, while exporters bought 3,400 bales, leaving 78,044 bales unsold at ginning factories. A total of 231 ginning factories were operational across the country, including 108 in Punjab and 123 in Sindh, although some units had either become partially operational or suspended operations due to financial and commercial constraints. Sanghar district alone accounted for 398,000 bales, representing 82pc of Sindh’s production and 51pc of the national total, largely because cotton sown in the province’s coastal areas during February and March had reached the market earlier than crops in other regions. Commenting on the latest arrivals, Sajid Mahmood, head of the Transfer of Technology Department at the Central Cotton Research Institute (CCRI), Multan, said the early-season figures suggested a stronger start than last year, particularly in Sindh where timely sowing, harvesting and improved crop management had supported higher arrivals. However, he cautioned that August and September would provide a more reliable indication of the overall crop. He also warned that prolonged humidity and rainfall could increase the incidence of whitefly, pink bollworm and crop diseases, affecting lint quality and yields. He advised growers to ensure proper drainage, closely monitor pest populations and adopt plant protection measures in consultation with agricultural extension officials. Greaves at that time was almost hobbling after a left leg injury sustained while completing a sharp run that necessitated lengthy treatment on the field. His sixth-wicket partnership with Roston Chase (36 not out) was worth 66 runs at the close of play and Greaves will be hoping for a return to his usual athletic mobility when he resumes the innings with his captain on the second morning. He had come to the crease in mid-afternoon at 91 for three as Kavem Hodge became Khan’s first wicket. When Amir Jangoo fell to a leg-side catch to give debutant pacer Ubaid Shah his first Test wicket with the first ball of a new spell, Pakistan sensed the chance to break the back of the West Indies resistance. Move to create over 100 new additional assistant commissioner posts in Punjab opposed

LAHORE: The Provincial Manage­ment Service (PMS) officers’ association has opposed the Punjab government’s decision to create 101 Additional Assistant Commissioner (AAC) posts, calling it a “mala fide move” aimed at “weakening” the PMS cadre. The association said the AAC posts were abolished decades ago and their revival was “unnecessary and unjustified”, especially after the establishment of the Punjab Enforcement and Regulatory Authority (PERA) force. The association’s executive committee meeting chaired by president Qamar Zaman Qaisrani decided that an association delegation would meet the Punjab services secretary and the additional chief secretary to explain its concerns and demands on Monday (today). “If PMSA concerns are not addressed, it will launch the first phase of protest by wearing black armbands across the province from Tuesday (tomorrow).” The association said that creating new posts would place an additional burden on the national exchequer and be seen as a direct attempt to undermine the PMS service structure. PMS officers announce protest, say decision ‘undermines’ their service structure Calculating the cost of an AAC and all 101 AACs, the association claims that one such officer would cost the government some Rs46.825 million and all 101 AACs some Rs4.729 billion at once, followed by monthly salaries and privileges. Besides salaries, the cost includes an official vehicle, supporting staff salaries, monthly budgets as well as an office building. The association also expressed serious concerns over alleged discrimination in postings and transfers, delays in promotion boards, and the appointment of junior officers to senior positions. Former administrative secretary Yawar Mehdi commented that the objective of creating AAC posts to improve public service delivery was commendable, provided these officers were empowered to deliver efficient, transparent, and citizen-centric services. However, he added that strengthening field administration should not come at the cost of weakening the civil secretariat or limiting the career progression of PMS officers. ISLAMABAD: Key lea­ders of coalition partners, reacting to Interior Min­ister Mohsin Naqvi’s proposal to overhaul the country’s governance stru­cture and create new provinces, urged him to first implement the National Action Plan (NAP), arguing that Pakistan’s governance fail­ures stem from constitutional violations rather than the existing federating units. I more or less personally agree with these views,” Mr Asif wrote, describing the interior minister as a “powerhouse”. He added that, as a political worker, he believed the country’s political evolution had been “held captive to factional and personal interests for decades” and needed to be freed. The federal minister advised Mr Naqvi to begin the overhaul process by implementing NAP through his own ministry. He also urged Mr Naqvi to bring about a “revolution” in the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB), which he heads in addition to serving as interior minister. Last week, Mr Naqvi had said the country’s governance system had “collapsed” and argued that Pakistan needed new administrative units, or provinces, to improve service delivery, accountability and access to justice. He also called for new leadership, saying the country’s youth wanted justice, merit and jobs rather than stipends and laptops. A day later, the military’s spokesperson also called on political parties to consider an administrative reset to improve governance, arguing that the country’s security and stability depended on adapting its governance structure to the demands of modern times and a growing population. Mr Asif said that if the system was to be str­engthened and meaningful reforms introduced, the appropriate forum was parliament, of which Mr Naqvi is a member, or the federal cabinet. “This way, these institutions and the system would be strengthened, and change would come through a constitutional process,” he said. The defence minister also pointed out that Mr Naqvi was a cabinet member but claimed he rar­ely attended parliamentary or cabinet sessions. “In my opinion, treating these institutions as irrelevant and making such a major statement before a captive audience after inviting the country’s eminent and respected businessmen does not carry the same weight that it would have had if made at the relevant constitutional forums,” he said. These include strict adherence to the supremacy of the Constitution, 1973; upholding the rule of law; ensuring full compliance with the provincial autonomy guaranteed under the Constitution, 1973; strict observance of the constitutional principle of the trichotomy of powers; and devolving political, administrative, and financial authority to elected local governments as envisaged under Article 140-A of the Constitution, 1973.
