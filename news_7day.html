However, the government took the PTI founder to the government-run Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences (Pims) for a check-up and then transported him back to prison. The SC’s office has already turned down Uzma’s two previous requests for an early hearing of the contempt plea, which was filed on Aug 22. The second early hearing request was submitted after the SC had fixed the main petition for Sept 16. However, on that day, the SC deferred the matter in the wake of the Federal Constitutional Court’s (FCC) directives. In both denials, the registrar office had stressed that the fixation policy required proceedings to be conducted turn-wise. It noted that 94 different criminal original petitions belonging to the same category were already pending before the SC for hearing. Subsequently, on Tuesday, a fresh application for early hearing was filed through Advocate Uzair Karamat Bhandari on behalf of Dr Uzma, following the PTI’s claim that Imran’s blood pressure surged above 190 recently. Moved under Order XXXV Rules 5 and 6 of the Supreme Court Rules, 2025, the application requests the SC to order fixation of the contempt plea for next week. The contempt plea had named Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Islamabad Chief Commissioner Sohail Ashraf, Interior Secretary Ahmed Raza Sarwar, Punjab Inspector General Prisons Mian Salik Jalal and Adiala jail’s Superintendent Sajid Baig as respondents. In the fresh application, Dr Uzma has stressed the urgency in the matter due to reports circulating in the media that the incarcerated leader’s blood pressure recently rose to 192 and that there were serious concerns about his health. “If an early hearing of this matter is not granted, the applicant’s and Imran Khan’s guaranteed rights will continue to be prejudiced,” the application stated, arguing that his fundamental rights, particularly with respect to health and well-being, as well as access to medical advice and facilities and family members, would continue to be violated. The application stated the previous contempt plea had also sought court directives to arrange and facilitate weekly meetings between Imran and his family members, including petitioners and his other sisters, Noreen Niazi and Aleema Khan, along with his cousin Qasim Zaman. It had also sought phone calls between Imran and his sons Kasim Khan and Sulaiman Khan twice a week. On Sept 16, resuming the hearing on petitions concerning Imran’s health, the SC had expressed reservations over the Federal Constitutional Court’s (FCC) directive that cases pertaining to the PTI founder’s hospital transfer be fixed before it. However, the SC had deferred its own proceedings, observing that propriety demanded deferment. At the time, the court said it was adjourning the proceedings for a date after three weeks, and is yet to be fixed for hearing. The FCC had issued the orders, directing the court’s office to requisition the relevant cases from the SC and fix them before it under Article 175-E of the Constitution. The directive was issued on a plea by three Adiala jail inmates seeking relief similar to that provided to Imran by the SC in the Aug 18 interim order. A three-judge SC bench, headed by Justice Shahid Waheed and including Justices Naeem Akhtar Afghan and Ishtiaq Ibrahim, had also observed that fixing cases before the FCC requisitioned from the SC did not fall under clause 5 of Article 175-E of the Constitution. Article 175-E, which was inserted in the Constitution through the 27th Constitutional Amendment, empowers the FCC to call for the record of any case and authorises it to decide matters involving major questions of constitutional law.

Britain says its bases are safe after US refuelling planes evacuated

Britain said on Tuesday that its military bases were safe, after the US removed huge refuelling aircraft from a British airbase following what the Wall Street Journal said was a threat from Iran, the second such apparent evacuation in three months. Commercially available flight tracking data showed at least 10 Boeing KC-135R Stratotanker refuelling planes left the Royal Air Force base in Mildenhall, eastern England, on Monday. One flew to a base in Germany, one to Scotland and two to Spain, while the destinations of the other six were not reported. At least three Osprey multipurpose aircraft also departed from Mildenhall and landed at Lakenheath, another RAF base 4 miles away. Video filmed by a member of the public from outside the base and verified by Reuters showed two Ospreys taxiing on the runway after landing at Lakenheath. Asked about the moves, the US European Command said the Air Force moves aircraft between locations for a variety of reasons, and it would not comment on force protection measures for operational security reasons. A spokesman for Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the protection of Britain’s military bases was a top priority, and that the government worked closely with the police and allies to protect them. Security measures were kept under constant review and the government was investing in air defence and counter-drone capabilities, he said. Asked if he was confident that Britain’s bases were safe, he said: “Yes.” The White House and the Pentagon did not immediately respond to requests for comment sent outside regular business hours. US removed bombers in September According to the Wall Street Journal, the evacuations were prompted by a threat from Iran. The precise nature of the threat was under discussion among US officials, it said. Iran has denied any links to specific attacks in Britain, but its Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps (IRGC) have said airbases used by US forces there and in other countries are legitimate targets. Britain has been warning for months that Iran has been planning attacks, recruiting people online to carry them out. In September, the US removed bombers that had been used to strike Iran from another British base, RAF Fairford, on security grounds after a group of men approached the base in white vans. Two men were charged last month in relation to that incident, with prosecutors alleging one of them had tried to assist Iranian intelligence in Britain. Britain’s domestic spy chief Ken McCallum said in a speech last month that Iran-linked operations had led to more than 50 arrests over the past year. Iran was ramping up attempts to hit harder targets such as military sites in the UK, even though its previous activity had been limited by the lack of sophistication of the criminals it used as proxies, he said. The US upgraded the security posture at RAF Mildenhall and another UK base, RAF Lakenheath, to FPCON Charlie, the second-highest defensive threat level, the Wall Street Journal report said, citing a US official familiar with the matter. The Fairford incident, which remains largely unexplained, attracted huge international attention at a time of heightened concern about the security implications for Western states of the war between the United States and Iran. Burnham has said the activity at RAF Fairford was “highly likely” to have been coordinated in Iran.

Centre spent Rs5tr on devolved subjects in violation of Constitution, Senate panel head says

ISLAMABAD: The federal government spent more than Rs5 trillion on devolved provincial subjects and on ministries created on Council of Common Interests (CCI) matters in violation of the Constitution, the head of a Senate panel claimed on Tuesday. Citing data from the report of the Auditor General of Pakistan, Senator Barrister Zamir Hussain Ghumro, chairing a sub-committee of the Senate Functional Committee on Devolution, said the figure was equivalent to more than $18 billion. The convener said the country was facing twin issues of exorbitant federal expenditure and heavy borrowing totalling $135bn. “At present, federal government expenditure stands at around Rs19 trillion, while total tax and non-tax revenue was Rs20tr, leaving nothing for the provinces and forcing the federal government to resort to heavy borrowing,” Ghumro said. The PPP senator contended that had the government implemented the 18th Amendment, devolved provincial matters and handed over CCI matters to the permanent secretariat of the CCI as envisaged under Article 154 of the Constitution, the country would not have needed handouts from the International Monetary Fund (IMF). During the last 15 years, the IMF gave Pakistan $15bn, while the government spent $18bn to $20bn on devolved ministries and institutions, he claimed. “Had this amount been spent on infrastructure and social development, Pakistan would have been a different country. “A taxpayer’s hard-earned money had been consigned to unauthorised expenditure,” he added. The convener said the federal government makes two kinds of expenditure from the Federal Consolidated Fund: “charged expenditure” on remuneration and other perks of the president, Senate chair and deputy chair, National Assembly speaker and deputy speaker, auditor general, high court and Supreme Court judges; and debt servicing, which the NA does not vote on, and “other expenditure” in the form of demands for grants by federal ministries created on federal subjects. “It must not spend a penny on provincial or other purposes,” Senator Ghumro stressed. “This is taxpayers’ money paid to the federal government to be spent for the purposes and services rendered by the federal government under the Constitution. “The federal government is not obliged to provide health, education, housing, sanitation, water or other services for which the Constitution empowers provincial governments.” Senator Ghumro added: “A citizen cannot pay the federal government for purposes and services not rendered by it under the Constitution; hence such unauthorised expenditure of more than Rs5tr amounts to gross misuse of authority under the law and Constitution and must be accounted for legally.” He said neither the Ministry of Finance can send demands for grants for such expenditure nor the auditor general or Accountant General of Pakistan can authorise it. They have to see that the expenditure is for purposes and services to be provided by the federal government under the Constitution, he said. “Those matters are specifically mentioned in the Federal Legislative List. All powers under Article 142 are vested in the provinces except those enumerated for the federation in the Federal List.” Senator Ghumro said that, under Article 154 (functions and rules of procedure of CCI) read with Article 97 (extent of executive authority of federation), executive authority over subjects mentioned in the Federal Legislative List’s Part II is to be exercised by the CCI and not the federal cabinet. The convener noted that the CCI and Cabinet Division had sent summaries to the prime minister to abolish 24 ministries and institutions, thus curtailing federal expenditure, but the prime minister had yet to respond. He said that, even otherwise, such ministries would become redundant as the Auditor General of Pakistan and the finance ministry would not allow such expenditure. He directed the CCI and Cabinet Division to send a fresh note to the prime minister for abolition of ministries on provincial and CCI matters. In July, the Functional Committee on Devolution had expressed serious concerns over the re-establishment of federal ministries on subjects devolved to the provinces under the 18th Amendment. It had also constituted a sub-committee to examine the alleged violations. During a meeting of that sub-committee last month, senators sought a probe into a staggering Rs2.6 trillion expenditure by the federal government on education and health ministries. Provinces’ share in federal appointments The sub-committee also took up the matter of shares of provinces in federal appointments in ministries and corporations. The Establishment Division informed the committee that, as per its instructions, 5,000 jobs of Balochistan had been advertised to rectify its share as required under Article 38(g) of the Constitution. Senator Ghumro said the committee had identified a shortage of more than 30,000 jobs in Balochistan’s share, which must be filled. The convener noted that a shortage of more than 80,000 jobs had been pointed out in Sindh, and that positions must be advertised to rectify it. The Federal Public Service Commission secretary informed the sub-committee that appointments for the shares of Sindh and Balochistan in services would be expedited as they had cleared the earlier backlog. He said a special CSS examination was conducted for these provinces. The sub-committee observed that there was a meagre number of appointments of secretaries, CEOs and other high positions from Sindh and Balochistan. The convener said Article 38(g) envisages shares in all grades, and the federal government must submit a grade-wise position in grades 18 to 22 in 15 days. It must expedite the process through ministries in grades 1-15 positions, by FPSC in grades 16 and 17 positions and in grades 18 to 22 by the federal government and the prime minister. Compliance must be substantive and practical, he said. The Senate panel called for implementation, rather than “statements of intent”. It also expressed displeasure over the non-submission of a report by the information ministry, which had sought a month to devolve print media organisations and functions at the federal level and develop a mechanism to give broadcasting rights to provinces under Article 159 of the Constitution. It directed the ministry to finalise all issues in 15 days and submit compliance.

Lahore’s PM2.5 level eight times WHO guideline, report says

LAHORE: Satellite data detected 16,714 likely crop-residue fires in Punjab between Oct 1 and Nov 9, while Lahore’s average PM2.5 concentration was eight times the World Health Organisation’s (WHO) guideline value, according to a report by the Pakistan Air Quality Initiative (PAQI) that questioned the link between Diwali fireworks in India and the city’s pollution. The report, released on Tuesday, said the number of likely crop-residue fires in Punjab, Pakistan, remained almost unchanged from the corresponding period last year. Lahore’s average PM2.5 concentration stood at 126 micrograms per cubic metre during the period, exceeding the 24-hour National Environmental Quality Standards (NEQS) on all 40 days for which valid data were available. The average was 11pc lower than in the corresponding period in 2025. Satellite instruments detected 18,825 fires across Punjab, Pakistan, 89 per cent of which were classified as likely crop-residue burning. The highest daily count was recorded on Nov 1, with 1,102 detections. The 24-hour National Environmental Quality Standards (NEQS) were exceeded on all 40 days for which valid data were available. The average concentration was 11pc lower than during the corresponding period in 2025. Sheikhupura recorded the highest number of fires at 2,559, followed by Jhang (2,192), Okara (2,062), Hafizabad (1,893) and Gujranwala (1,417). The five districts accounted for 54pc of the total detections. The report found a positive correlation between upwind fire activity and PM2.5 levels in Lahore. On days when air trajectories passed over areas where burning was taking place, the average concentration reached 175 micrograms per cubic metre, compared with 93 micrograms on other days. A similar pattern was observed in Multan, where average PM2.5 levels reached 203 micrograms on days influenced by fires, compared with 88 micrograms on other days. In Bahawalpur, the corresponding figures were 125 and 65 micrograms. However, the report cautioned that the association did not establish the precise contribution of crop-residue burning to urban pollution, as stagnant weather conditions and reduced atmospheric dispersion also contributed to the accumulation of pollutants. The number of likely crop-residue fires in Punjab, Pakistan, was 8pc above the 2020-2025 average, although the comparable satellite record showed a 1pc decline from 2025. The assessment also examined whether fireworks during Diwali celebrations in India on Nov 8 had contributed to deteriorating air quality in Punjab. It found that easterly winds carried air from Indian territory towards Pakistan during the night. Of the 48 back trajectories arriving in Lahore, Kasur and Sialkot between 9pm on Nov 8 and midnight on Nov 10, 45 had passed over Indian territory within the preceding 12 hours, most frequently near Tarn Taran, Amritsar and Jammu. However, the report said the available evidence did not support attributing Lahore’s poor air quality after Diwali to fireworks across the border. Lahore’s average PM2.5 concentration between 6pm on Nov 8 and 1am on Nov 10 was 234 micrograms per cubic metre, 10pc higher than the average recorded during the same hours over the preceding seven days. In comparison, the average concentration in Kasur increased by 27pc and in Sialkot by 33pc, while Narowal recorded a 2pc decline. The increase was greater in cities farther from the international border. Faisalabad, located 130 kilometres from the border, recorded a 40pc increase, while Multan, 180km away, also saw a 40pc rise. The average increase in the four border cities — Lahore, Kasur, Sialkot and Narowal — was 36 micrograms per cubic metre, compared with 85 micrograms in Faisalabad and Multan. The report noted that pollution transported from across the border would be expected to cause a greater increase in border areas than in cities farther inland. However, the observed pattern did not support that explanation. Sialkot was an exception, recording a 37pc increase in PM2.5 during the night after Diwali, with air trajectories passing over the Jammu area. The assessment also found that emissions from New Delhi had not reached Lahore during the period analysed. Air from Delhi entered Pakistan near Rahim Yar Khan and Bahawalpur only after 3am on Nov 10, with its closest approach to Lahore remaining 266 kilometres away. The report said distinguishing between emissions from fireworks and smoke from agricultural fires required chemical analysis of particulate matter, including elements such as potassium, strontium and barium, which Pakistani monitoring stations did not measure. Egerton Road recorded the biggest improvement, with its reading falling from 500 to 167, followed by GT Road, where the AQI dropped from 500 to 172. A special assessment issued on Nov 8 had cited 2,347 incidents of crop-residue burning in Indian Punjab up to Nov 4. Of these, 1,081 were recorded in the districts of Tarn Taran, Ferozepur and Amritsar. It had also warned that smoke from Diwali fireworks could reach Lahore within five to 13 hours, depending on wind conditions. EPCCD Secretary Silwat Saeed said the department was responding to deteriorating air quality through coordinated enforcement, real-time environmental monitoring and targeted field interventions. She said the department was using its monitoring infrastructure and Punjab Climate Watch data to identify pollution hotspots and direct operational teams to areas requiring immediate action. Environment Protection Agency (EPA) Director General Abdullah Nayyar Sheikh said the department had adopted a zero-tolerance approach towards major sources of air pollution. He said Quick Response Units were responding to satellite-detected fire anomalies and reports of crop-residue burning, with field teams being dispatched to extinguish fires and take action against those responsible. He added that inspections of factories, brick kilns and other emission-generating units had been intensified, while anti-smog gun operations were being carried out at full capacity in Lahore. The director general said the improvement recorded at all 11 monitoring stations was encouraging, but enforcement, surveillance and mitigation operations would continue.
