‘Grand dialogue’

THE country’s top civil and military leaders are absolutely right when they say that dialogue and development are needed to address Balochistan’s crises. But perhaps one should recall that many earlier attempts by the state to bring peace to the province were stalled or remain unimplemented. In order to truly stabilise Balochistan and bring lasting peace to this troubled province, a credible political process must begin, with the state guaranteeing that it will fully implement previous promises, as well as new agreements. While speaking at the Balochistan National Workshop in Islamabad recently, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif called for a “grand, frank and meaningful dialogue” to address the province’s issues. He added that the province’s people had first right over its resources. In a similar vein the chief of defence forces stressed the need for empowering Balochistan’s youth. It is certainly welcome that the rulers see the need to tackle Balochistan’s political problems through dialogue. While kinetic measures are needed to deal with immediate terrorist threats, the underlying factors fuelling discontent can only be handled through a political process. The state must keep in mind previous attempts to address the province’s problems. For example, the PPP government had in 2009 launched the Aghaz-i-Huqooq-i-Balochistan package. Despite the passage of 17 years, many of the package’s key points remain unimplemented. For example, it had proposed releasing political workers, except those involved in heinous crimes. However, over the past few months, we have seen peaceful activists rounded up and thrown in jail. Similarly, the 2009 package had sought to address the province’s socioeconomic woes; suffice it to say, social indicators in Balochistan remain miserable. The PML-N had launched a package of its own in 2015. Much of that scheme also remains unfulfilled. CPEC was similarly supposed to transform the province’s fortunes. It has not. Instead of reinventing the wheel, the state must implement what it had promised in these packages. Many of these schemes’ salient points reflect the demands of the province’s nationalist politicians. Therefore, to implement these promises in full, a grand dialogue should bring on board the province’s genuine political leadership, as well as peaceful activists, while a verifiable and transparent mechanism needs to be worked out to help implement previous and new promises. The past few months have seen a sharp spike in terrorist activity in Balochistan. The only sustainable way of addressing the insurgency is to supplement kinetic actions with a genuine political process that can, in the long-term, eliminate the underlying factors that are fuelling discontent. Comstech chief remains defiant in face of ‘damning inquiry’

• President-ordered probe found OIC official guilty of ‘misconduct, non-compliance’• Accused official calls inquiry ‘one-sided’, claims Presidency was ‘misused’ ISLAMABAD: An inquiry committee formed on the directives of President Asif Ali Zardari has declared the top official of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation’s (OIC) Standing Committee on Scientific and Technological Cooperation (Comstech) guilty of multiple charges of misconduct. According to the inquiry report seen by Dawn, Comstech Coordinator General Dr Iqbal Choudhry was accused of financial irregularities, violation of executive orders and non-compliance with court directives. However, the official in question has questioned the legality of proceedings against him, calling it a “one-sided inquiry”. According to the inquiry report, Dr Choudhry was initially appointed by then-president Arif Alvi in 2020 during the PTI’s tenure, and was subsequently reappointed by President Zardari for another four-year term in October 2024. Under Articles 12(2), read with 16(3) of the Comstech statute, the President of Pakistan is the appointing authority for the post of the body’s coordinator general. Comstech was created in January 1981 at the Third Islamic Summit in Makkah, Saudi Arabia. Headquartered in Islamabad, Pakistan, it aims to promote science, technology and research cooperation among OIC member states. The inquiry committee was headed by Dr Asim Hussain, the chancellor of Ziauddin University. Its members included Pakistan Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (PCSIR) Chairman Dr Syed Hussain Abidi, Ministry of Science and Technology Secretary Shahid Iqbal Baloch and its Chief Finance and Accounts Officer Muhammad Saeedullah Yousafzai. Inquiry findings The committee held that Dr Choudhry had failed to implement instructions issued by the president, terming the alleged failure “misconduct”. It also accused him of violating court orders, and failing to reply to complaints. The inquiry found that meetings of the Comstech Executive Committee had not been convened for the last four years, and that most decisions were taken by Dr Choudhry alone, terming this “maladministration”. It further maintained that expenditures had been incurred for several years without budgetary approval, which the committee declared “unauthorised and illegal”. The report recommended strict disciplinary action over the alleged misuse of Comstech funds, including expenditure allegedly made for personal benefit. The committee alleged that Dr Choudhry undertook more than 70 foreign trips over the last six years without prior approval from the president in his capacity as Comstech chairman. Some of the visits, it said, were to countries not associated with Comstech, while funds were also allegedly used to cover the travel and accommodation expenses of female associates. The report also pointed out that Dr Choudhry simultaneously held the posts of Comstech coordinator general and director of the International Centre for Chemical and Biological Sciences (ICCBS) from April 8, 2020, to Nov 12, 2024, a period of around four and a half years, despite directives from the ministry to relinquish the additional charge. The inquiry also cited complaints regarding forced resignations and dismissals of employees, a lack of transparent procedures in disciplinary proceedings, and allegations of nepotism and favouritism in recruitment. It noted that the World Academy of Sciences, Trieste, Italy, had also issued a reprimand letter to Dr Choudhry. The inquiry committee has recommended that the matter be referred to anti-corruption institutions for further investigation and appropriate action against Dr Choudhry on multiple counts of alleged misconduct. The recommendations in the inquiry report have been submitted to the competent authority for further proceedings. Official’s response When approached for comment, Dr Choudhry told Dawn he was unaware of any such report, but raised questions over the legality of the inquiry, noting that procedural requirements had not been fulfilled. He claimed that neither any complaint nor a show cause notice was shared with him, and said that there were “serious procedural flaws in this one-sided inquiry”. “You cannot do it on the basis of a fake complaint,” he remarked. He maintained that the president, in his capacity as Comstech chairman, draws his powers from OIC procedures, and could not as as head of the state take a unilateral action against an OIC body which enjoys diplomatic immunity. Alleging that the Presidency was being misused, he said any inquiry or report prepared without proof or documentation has no legal or ethical standing, and is liable to be challenged in court.
