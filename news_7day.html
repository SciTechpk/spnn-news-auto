KP Governor Kundi announces amended welfare scheme for police officers, martyrs’ families

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi on Friday announced changes to the compensation packages for police officers martyred or injured in the line of duty as part of efforts to promote peace in the province. The provincial government has increased compensation for the families of martyred police officers and amended the previous plot allotment scheme, opting to pay the families cash in lieu of plots. Under the new scheme, the families of martyred constables, head constables, assistant sub-inspectors (ASIs), sub-inspectors (SIs) and inspectors will receive Rs17 million in compensation, up from Rs11 million. For the families of martyred deputy superintendents of police (DSPs) and assistant superintendents of police (ASPs), compensation will increase from Rs16 million to Rs25 million. The families of martyred superintendents of police (SP), senior superintendents of police (SSP), assistant inspector general (AIG) and all grade 18 and 19 officers previously received Rs21m in compensation, but under the new scheme, they will receive Rs30m. For families of martyred deputy inspector generals (DIG), inspector generals of police (IGP) and grade 20 officers, the compensation would increase from Rs21m to Rs35m. Regarding compensation for injured police officers, Kundi said an increase should have been made earlier, but it had not been. To address this, he said, the government had made “significant increases” in compensation for officers who had been severely injured or permanently disabled in the line of duty. Previously, compensation for permanent disability stood at Rs500,000. His government had increased it tenfold to Rs5 million. Compensation for severe injuries had also been increased from Rs100,000 to Rs1.5m. Kundi added that the families of martyred police officers had previously been allotted plots. However, the scheme had proved inconvenient for some families, as those living in Dera Ismail Khan or Bannu might be allotted plots in Nowshera or Swabi. Under the new scheme, the families would receive financial compensation in lieu of plots. He stressed that cash payments would give the families the freedom to decide how to use the money, whether to rent a house or buy a plot or a house in a location of their choice. For the families of martyred constables and head constables, the government will provide Rs18 million in cash in lieu of a five-marla plot. The families of martyred ASIs, SIs and inspectors will receive Rs25m in cash instead of a seven-marla plot. Kundi further said that the families of DSPs and ASPs, who were previously entitled to a 10-marla plot, would now receive Rs30m in cash. For the families of officers of SP or SSP rank and above, compensation had been set at Rs70m in cash in lieu of a one-kanal plot. Kundi stressed that not only serving police officers but also their families were at risk. Noting that terrorists could target officers’ families, he said the government had decided to introduce a new compensation package for such incidents. If a police officer’s spouse or children were killed, the family would receive Rs5m in compensation. If an officer’s parents or siblings were killed, the government would pay Rs3m in compensation. Kundi also announced a compensation package for police officers who were killed while off duty. The families of constables, head constables, ASIs and inspectors killed while off duty would receive Rs4m. The compensation for the families of DSPs and ASPs killed while off duty had been set at Rs5m. For the families of SPs, SSPs and officers in grades 18 and 19, compensation had been set at Rs7m. The KP government has also announced Rs1m in compensation to help fund the weddings of martyred officers’ daughters, describing it as “essential financial assistance at an important stage of the families’ lives”, Kundi said. Children of martyred officers will also benefit from a new fund being established to support their education, including studies abroad, Kundi said. The governor added that salaries of KP police officers had been increased by more than expected in an effort to make their pay packages more attractive than those offered in other provinces. He added that he had directed the relevant officials to expedite recruitment to the KP police, which has 17,000 vacancies. Concluding his remarks, Kundi said: “The days of rifts between the Centre and the province are gone. We now have to work together for the province’s peace and prosperity.” Kundi had on Tuesday directed the authorities to prepare and submit, at the earliest, a summary containing proposals for an increase in police salaries and an enhanced welfare package for the families of police martyrs. Chairing a high-level meeting at the Governor House, he reviewed proposals relating to police salaries and welfare measures for the families of martyred and injured police personnel. On Oct 11, the federal government imposed emergency and governor’s rule in KP, under Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution, making Kundi the leader of the province.

PHC to hear PTI chairman’s plea against emergency, governor’s rule in KP on Oct 20

PESHAWAR: The Peshawar High Court on Friday fixed October 20 for the preliminary hearing of a petition filed by PTI Chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan challenging the imposition of emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and seeking to have the move declared unconstitutional. The federal government on October 11 imposed emergency in KP, and placed the province under governor’s rule, taking note of the “provincial government’s deliberate disengagement from the fight against terrorism”. The move followed weeks of political tensions between the Centre and the PTI — as well as negotiations to bridge their differences — after the latter announced a long march to Islamabad to demand the release of its jailed founder, Imran Khan. The opposition had criticised the move, vowing to raise its voice at every forum, including the judiciary. The instant petition was filed on October 14 by the PTI chairman. He will be represented by a panel of lawyers, including Barrister Sameer Khosa, Salman Akram Raja, Ali Zaman, Sardar Haseeb Ahmad and Zainab Shahid. The members of the bench that will hear the petition will be announced later. In his petition, Gohar urged the PHC to declare the federal government’s decision to impose emergency and governor’s rule in KP “unconstitutional, ultra vires, coram non judice, tainted with malice in law and fact, devoid of objective material, and of no legal effect” and to set aside the proclamation. Filed under Article 199 of the Constitution, the petition named the federation of Pakistan (through the secretaries of the Cabinet Division, law, interior, and defence ministries); the province of KP; KP Governor Faisal Karim Kundi; the KP Assembly speaker; and the secretary of the KP Home and Tribal Affairs Department as respondents. Sohail Afridi, who is no longer functioning as the province’s chief minister following the imposition of governor’s rule, was also named as a pro forma respondent. Gohar challenged the “constitutionality, legality and vires” of the October 11 decision, the order deploying army troops to the province, and “all consequential and derivative executive orders, notifications, and bodies” constituted as a result. These included the establishment of the KP Advisory Council and the “arbitrary” detention and removal of Afridi as the lawful chief minister of KP, along with his provincial cabinet, which had been vested with full executive powers under Article 130 of the Constitution. Following the imposition of emergency and governor’s rule in the province, the federal government formed an 11-member technocratic KP Advisory Council. The council will assist Governor Kundi, who is now in charge of running the province, in exercising the functions of the KP government on behalf of the federal government. The PHC was further requested to declare that Paragraph 2 of the proclamation — which transferred the legislative powers of the KP Assembly to Parliament in the absence of a prior resolution passed by the assembly, as required under Article 232(1) — was “void ab initio and an unconstitutional usurpation of provincial autonomy”. The petition also urged the PHC to restrain the federation from “tabling, entertaining, or enacting any provincial legislative business concerning Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in Parliament”. The petitioner asked the court to declare the proclamation illegal and unconstitutional and to set aside, dissolve or permanently restrain all executive orders, notifications, actions and bodies created “pursuant to or under the colour of” the proclamation. These included the constitution of the 11-member KP Advisory Council, headed by Governor Kundi; the establishment of the Area Development Secretariat (ADS) across 12 districts of the province; and the removal of the KP advocate general and the purported appointment of a replacement. The ADS was established to “restore law and order, safeguard the security of the citizens of Pakistan and ensure good governance and development” in Dera Ismail Khan, Tank, Lakki Marwat, Bannu, Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, Kurram, Orakzai, North Waziristan, South Waziristan (Upper) and South Waziristan (Lower). The petitioner also challenged the October 11 order deploying army troops under Article 245, asking the court to declare it unconstitutional on the grounds that it could not “supplant or bypass the civilian executive and constitutional administration” of the province and could not be invoked in the absence of a lawful requisition by the provincial government headed by Afridi. Gohar sought directives requiring the respondents, including the federal government and the KP governor, to immediately disclose, produce and place before the PHC “the entire record and so-called ‘objective material’”, including Governor Kundi’s report and the federal cabinet’s summary, on which the emergency proclamation and subsequent orders were allegedly based. He also sought directives for the immediate release of Afridi and members of the KP cabinet and assembly from “unlawful executive or preventive detention”. The petition further urged the court to direct the respondents to immediately produce Afridi before the PHC and restrain the state from placing him or any cabinet members in preventive detention or transferring them outside the court’s jurisdiction. The petition claimed that, by unilaterally bypassing the elected provincial assembly over an alleged question of internal disturbance, the impugned proclamation violated “the federal structure guaranteed under Article 1, the principles of democracy and provincial autonomy enshrined in the Objectives Resolution (Article 2A), and the legislative domain of the province recognised by Article 142 of the Constitution”. On these grounds, the proclamation was “ultra vires the proviso to Article 232(1) read with Article 1, 2A, and 142 of the Constitution” and liable to be set aside, it added. Gohar further contended that the proclamation made no mention of any “grave emergency” arising from a war or other external aggression, as required by the text of Article 232(1) — nor would such an assertion be supported by facts. He maintained that neither the country nor KP faced a grave emergency from any ongoing war or external aggression, nor was there any known risk or threat of such. The petition further contended that the reliance on “internal disturbances” in the province needed to be assessed against a demonstrable and objective criterion, which was “entirely absent” from the impugned proclamation. “Despite an unfortunately high number of incidents of terrorist violence in Balochistan, no state of emergency has been declared or even contemplated,” the petition stated. “This clearly demonstrates that the terrorism and security pretext in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is nothing but a colourable device, deployed in sheer violation of Article 25.” The petition alleged that this pretext had been used solely because the KP government was formed by the opposition and its demands regarding PTI founder Imran Khan were unacceptable to the federal government. It added that the federal government’s claim was also belied by the fact that even at the height of terrorism between 2007 and 2015, “no emergency was ever imposed anywhere inside Pakistan”. Gohar also argued that the invocation of Article 234 was “without lawful authority, tainted with malice in law, and devoid of jurisdictional basis”. NDM petition Another petition challenging the imposition of emergency, filed by National Democratic Movement (NDM) central and provincial presidents Mohsin Dawar and Bushra Gohar, respectively, has also been fixed for hearing on October 20. The joint petition stated that the proclamation of emergency and governor’s rule was “purely and patently against the mandate of people of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, hence, liable to be set at naught”. The petitioners prayed that the court declare the proclamation issued by the president of Pakistan on October 11, 2026, to be without legal effect and jurisdiction. They also requested the court to set aside the directions, appointments and actions taken, as well as omissions made, pursuant to the proclamation, to ensure constitutional conformity. They further sought interim relief, asking the court to suspend the operation of the impugned presidential proclamation until the final disposal of the writ petition. The petitioners stated that the president of Pakistan was under a solemn constitutional duty, under Article 42 of the Constitution read with the Third Schedule, to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and not allow personal interests to influence official conduct or decisions. They claimed that the KP governor, acting with mala fide intent and without jurisdiction, submitted a tailor-made report to the president of Pakistan. They alleged that, without taking into account the constitutional guidelines explicitly set out in Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution of Pakistan, 1973, the respondents issued the impugned proclamation on October 11, 2026. The petitioners contended that the KP government and its executive had never reported the respondent emergency call/whistle blow regarding the security concern, which was beyond the control of the government; hence, the action and issuance of the proclamation solely and exclusively by the president was against the norms and contours of democracy and provincial autonomy. They added that the issuance of the proclamation was the outcome of mala fide, and patently without jurisdiction and had no constitutional backing. They further contended that Article 232 of the Constitution had delineated a specific procedure and limitations for declaring and proclamation of emergency in the province, which had not been followed by the respondents; hence, the action and orders of “Proclamation of Emergency” in the province of KP were unconstitutional and unlawful.

18 countries, including Pakistan, condemn inauguration of Colombian embassy in Jerusalem

Eighteen countries, including Pakistan, on Friday strongly condemned the opening of the Colombian embassy in Jerusalem, stating that any move to recognise the city as Israel’s capital had no legal effect. Colombia on Wednesday officially opened its embassy in Jerusalem, joining a small group of countries including the United States in taking the symbolic step of backing Israeli sovereignty in the contested city. The vast majority of countries keep their embassies to Israel in Tel Aviv on the grounds that the status of Jerusalem will be settled in a final resolution of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. In a joint statement shared by the Foreign Office (FO), the foreign ministers of Pakistan, Algeria, Djibouti, Egypt, Indonesia, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Malaysia, Oman, Palestine, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, Syria, Sudan and Turkiye said they “strongly condemn and express their unequivocal rejection of the inauguration by the Republic of Colombia of its embassy in Jerusalem”. The statement said the development was in contravention of relevant United Nations Security Council (UNSC) resolutions and undermined international efforts to achieve a “just and lasting peace based on the two-state solution”. “The foreign ministers reaffirmed that any measure or decision intended to alter the legal or historical status of occupied Jerusalem, or to recognise it as Israel’s capital, is null and void and has no legal effect,” the joint statement said. “This position is consistent with relevant UNSC and General Assembly resolutions, particularly Security Council resolutions 476 (1980) and 478 (1980), which reject attempts to change Jerusalem’s character or status and call on states to withdraw diplomatic missions from the city, which is part of the Palestinian territory occupied in 1967,” it added. As per the joint statement, the foreign ministers called upon the Colombian government to “reconsider and reverse this decision, uphold its obligations under international law, and refrain from measures that prejudice the legitimate rights of the Palestinian people, including their right to an independent and sovereign Palestinian State with East Jerusalem as its capital”. In May this year, Pakistan, along with 13 other countries, also condemned the “illegal and unacceptable step” of the so-called Somaliland opening a “purported” embassy in occupied Jerusalem, terming it an “infringement on the legal and historical status of Jerusalem”. US President Donald Trump, during his first term, moved the US embassy to Jerusalem and recognised the city as Israel’s capital. Since Trump’s move, only a handful of small, US-aligned countries have followed suit — Fiji, Guatemala, Honduras, Papua New Guinea, Paraguay and Nauru. Kosovo and Somaliland, which are not member-states of the United Nations, have also opened what they consider their embassies in Jerusalem.

The minister also met Carlos Carvallo Spalding, governor of Paraguay’s central bank and the outgoing chair of the Boards of Governors, according to the statement. Google boosts news and link visibility in AI mode

Google announced on Thursday that it will start displaying links to websites and articles more prominently and higher up in search results powered by artificial intelligence (AI), as brands and news publishers warn of a sharp decline in their traffic. The Alphabet subsidiary began integrating more AI features in its search results over two years ago. In May 2024, it launched AI Overviews, where Google generates summarised answers to search queries, rather than only showing links as it historically did. Then, in March 2025, Google added AI Mode, which opens in a separate window and provides a more comprehensive answer and more links than AI Overviews. Now, contextual links will be featured more “more frequently” and higher up in AI-generated search content. Google will also expand worldwide its link “carousel,” which is cards displayed directly below AI Overviews that show headlines with images for relevant articles. The algorithm will prioritise articles from sites a user has previously identified as “preferred sources,” and it will also be able to pull in content from relevant social media posts and other forums. Google will also make links displayed at the end of AI-generated summaries “more engaging and prominent” for users who want to “dig deeper.” During its second-quarter 2025 results, Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai said that AI Overviews had more than two billion monthly users in over 200 countries. Several studies have shown that replacing traditional search with AI summaries significantly reduces the rate at which users open the suggested links. According to a report by consultancy Chartbeat, news website publishers saw a 40 per cent drop in traffic coming from Google between July 2025 and July 2026. In August, Apig, a group representing daily newspapers in France, alerted the Competition Authority that AI summaries were in breach of agreements reached with news publishers in 2022.
