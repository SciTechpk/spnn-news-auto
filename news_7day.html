Imaan, Hadi move SC against IHC order refusing to stay trial in security breach case

ISLAMABAD: Incarcerated human rights lawyer Imaan Mazari and her husband, Hadi Ali Chattha, on Monday approached the Supreme Court to challenge the Islamabad High Court’s (IHC) Oct 19 order refusing to stay the trial before an anti-terrorism court (ATC) in the security breach case. The case pertains to the alleged disruption of the security route of an international cricket team and incitement to public unrest. The couple, accused of removing barriers from the route, was indicted on October 7. On Oct 19, the IHC took up appeals filed by Imaan and Hadi against the Oct 10 ATC order that rejected their petitions challenging the framing of charges and the inclusion of terrorism-related provisions. While issuing notices, the IHC had said it would decide how to proceed once the parties’ responses were received. Subsequently, the couple, through senior counsel Faisal Siddiqui, filed separate near-identical petitions before the SC on Monday, challenging the IHC’s Oct 19 order. The plea argued that the trial court proceeded with unprecedented haste by recording the examination-in-chief of six prosecution witnesses on Oct 10 and 14, and accepting the prosecution’s request to shift the trial to jail. The decision to conduct the trial in jail had also been challenged before the IHC, with the petition pointing out that the trial court had appointed state counsel. The petitions alleged that the ongoing trial was nothing but persecution aimed at subverting the SC’s order suspending the petitioners’ 17-year sentences in the controversial social media post case and preventing their eventual release in an allegedly frivolous and mala fide case that had mysteriously resurfaced to prevent their release. Imaan’s petition argued that the IHC had “erroneously refused to exercise its discretion to grant ad interim relief to stay the criminal trial, despite multiple factual and legal reasons warranting such relief and repeated requests”. It said it was settled law that ad interim relief could not be denied in the presence of a prima facie case and where the balance of convenience favoured the petitioner, as she would otherwise suffer irreparable loss. The petition pleaded that Imaan had a clear prima facie case because recording evidence in their absence violated Section 353 (evidence to be taken in presence of accused) of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), as well as their due process and fair trial rights guaranteed under the law. It further argued that the balance of convenience favoured the petitioner because delaying the trial for some time would not prejudice the prosecution. The plea claimed that the petitioner would suffer irreparable loss given the alleged gross violations of due process and fair trial principles in the proceedings, adding that it was apparent that the trial was leading to her criminal conviction. The petitioner’s liberty was at stake because of the denial of ad interim relief, the petition contended, adding that the IHC’s order was without jurisdiction, illegal and liable to be set aside. It further pointed out that the trial court had never dispensed with the petitioner’s personal attendance during the recording of evidence and that no proceedings under Section 540-A (power to summon material witness or examine person present) of the CrPC had been initiated. The trial court’s order of Oct 10 recorded that counsel had left the rostrum and the accused had left the video link. The Oct 14 order also noted that the accused had again moved away from the camera while their counsel had left the rostrum during the examination-in-chief of witnesses. Despite this, the court proceeded to record the examination-in-chief of six prosecution witnesses on the two dates. The petitions emphasised that the absence of the petitioners and their counsel was forced as a protest and had been recorded as such, but did not amount to a waiver of the right to be present during the recording of evidence. IHC hears plea against jail trial, appointment of state counsel Meanwhile, the IHC heard arguments on a petition filed by Imaan and Hadi challenging their trial at Adiala jail and the appointment of a state counsel. A division bench comprising Justice Raja Inaam Ameen Minhas and Justice Shahrukh Arjumand continued the proceedings, with advocates Riasat Ali Azad and Zainab Janjua appearing for the petitioners. During the proceedings, Advocate Azad noted that the decision to conduct the trial inside the jail had been challenged before the IHC. He contended that the accused should have been heard before such an arrangement was approved. Justice Minhas questioned the legal basis of the challenge, asking who had the authority to decide the venue of a trial and whether an accused could dictate where the proceedings should take place. In response, Azad said the petitioners were being brought before what he described as a “jail court”, effectively a cage, where access to the proceedings was restricted. He argued that the summary for conducting the jail trial had been initiated by an Islamabad superintendent of police and subsequently approved by the chief commissioner. According to him, the police officer lacked the authority to initiate such a summary, which he maintained fell within the federal government’s domain. The judge questioned whether a summary concerning a jail trial would have to be placed before the cabinet and also asked about the legal concept of an open court. Azad said he had not been heard before the decision was taken, despite the need to hear the defence. He pointed out that journalists and members of the public were not permitted to attend the proceedings inside the jail. He further submitted that the defence had requested the trial judge to pass a formal judicial order regarding the arrangement, but the judge declined, telling the lawyers to challenge the matter before the high court. The lawyer also objected to the appointment of a state counsel during the jail trial. Justice Minhas observed that the defence lawyers’ vakalatnamas (powers of attorney authorising legal representation) were still on the court record. Advocate Janjua clarified that she had initially represented the accused but subsequently withdrew her vakalatnama, explaining that travelling to Adiala jail every day was incompatible with managing her legal practice. Azad added that travelling to and from the jail took around two hours each way. When the judge again asked whether his vakalatnama remained on record, Azad said the defence had effectively been sidelined. He alleged that when he sought a single adjournment, a state counsel was appointed and the cross-examination of prosecution witnesses was conducted in his absence. Questioning the urgency of the proceedings, Azad said the first information report (FIR) had remained concealed for two years and that the accused had been arrested in the case immediately after securing bail from the SC. He further contended that although the original charges were bailable, a terrorism-related charge had subsequently been added. The counsel requested the IHC to restrain the trial court from delivering its final verdict until the petition was decided. Justice Minhas observed that the accused could also be acquitted, asking the lawyer why he was seeking such an order. State counsel Ali Azad reiterated the request that the trial court be restrained from announcing its final verdict. After hearing the arguments, Justice Minhas remarked that the court would examine the matter. The bench did not announce a final decision on the request during the proceedings.

Central bank raises policy rate by 50 basis points to 12pc

The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) on Monday raised the key policy rate by 50 basis points to 12 per cent, its first increase since April as inflation, fuelled by high energy costs due to the Gulf conflict, remained above target. In a statement, the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) said it had decided to increase the policy rate, with a majority decision of nine out of ten members. It noted that the “intensification in the Middle East conflict and low prospects of its near-term resolution” were keeping global energy prices at persistently elevated levels. “While the impact of this global shock on the domestic economy has remained contained so far amidst proactive monetary policy adjustment and continued fiscal discipline, geopolitical developments have worsened the inflation outlook as compared to the earlier assessment,” it said. It went on to say that although the external account and growth outlook remained broadly unchanged, risks had also increased. “Considering these developments and the evolving risks, the MPC deemed today’s decision as necessary to keep inflation expectations anchored, contain second-round effects of the ongoing supply shock and bring inflation within the target range of 5-7pc. This will help preserve macroeconomic stability, which is necessary for sustainable economic growth,” it said. It said that forex reserves had remained stable at around $21bn, Pakistan had reached a staff-level agreement with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and that real gross domestic product (GDP) growth had been revised at 3.7pc, almost unchanged from the initial estimate. It further said that the Federal Board of Revenue had exceeded its revenue collection target during the first quarter of the current fiscal year, major central banks had raised interest rates in the wake of rising inflationary pressures and the IMF had also revised its global inflation projections upwards. “While noting these developments and the increasing frequency of global supply shocks, the MPC emphasized the importance of increasing external and fiscal buffers to further strengthen the economy’s resilience. The committee also reiterated that continuing the prudent monetary and fiscal policy mix, alongside timely implementation of structural reforms, is essential for achieving higher and sustainable economic growth,” the statement said. Regarding inflation, which eased to 10.3pc in September from 11pc in Augus, the MPC said that the re-escalation in the Middle East conflict and domestic wheat supply frictions were two major factors keeping inflation at elevated levels It said that their expected continuation over the next few months had “worsened the inflation outlook”. It added that there was a risk that elevated food and energy inflation may further increase inflation expectations, which may seep into core inflation going forward. “In view of these developments and increasing risks to the inflation outlook, the committee assessed today’s decision as important to keep the real policy rate adequately positive on forward-looking basis. This is necessary to bring inflation within the target range over the medium term. The inflation outlook is subject to multiple risks, including geopolitical tensions, global commodity price volatility, unanticipated adjustments in energy tariffs, further uptick in wheat and allied product prices and adverse weather conditions,” it said. Regarding the external sector, the statement said that exports were recovering, adding that remittances had remained resilient and were projected to turn out higher than the earlier assessment. It said that the current account deficit in FY27 was assessed to remain within the earlier projected range of 0-1pc of GDP. “This, along with higher financial inflows, is expected to increase SBP’s FX reserves beyond the current level. This outlook, however, remains susceptible to multiple risks, including elevated global commodity prices, tightening international financial conditions, and supply constraints,” it said. As per the statement, the committee expected GDP growth to remain within previously projected ranges of 3.5pc to 4.5pc in FY27. However, it added that the outlook was susceptible to risks from escalation in the Middle East conflict and adverse climate events such as El Niño conditions. SBP governor cites inflationary outlook SBP Governor Jameel Ahmed also addressed a press conference to announce the decision and said there were many “underlying factors” behind the decision, especially the inflation outlook. He cited the US-Israel war on Iran and its impact on energy and oil prices, developments in the domestic market and the subsequent impact on inflation. He said that nine out of ten MPC members voted to raise the policy rate, while one member had a different view; they were of the opinion that it should be raised further. He said that consumer expectations and other forecasters’ views were taken into consideration, as well as other macro factors, especially the impact of global commodity and oil prices on the external account and the impact of remittances and export trends. Ahmed noted a deterioration in the current account since the last MPC meeting. However, he added: “The good thing is that its impact is still between zero and one per cent of GDP and it will stay between that.” He also shared that the central bank revised its outlook on remittances, from $44 billion to $45bn. On exports, the SBP governor said the trend was witnessing an improvement; however, it was not yet in line with the economy’s needs. He added that the bank estimated imports to be close to $71bn. He said it would take longer than planned for inflation to get within the target range and revised GDP growth to 3.7pc for the current fiscal year ending June 30, 2027. Meanwhile, Topline Securities said the central bank had raised the policy rate “in line with our expectations”. Previously, the policy rate was 11.5pc. The central bank had held the rate steady since raising it by 100 basis points in April in response to rising global energy prices and supply chain risks — its first increase in nearly three years. The SBP has cut the key rate by a cumulative 1,050 basis points since mid-2024, from a record 22pc reached in June 2023, as inflation cooled sharply from multi-decade highs. Last week, the IMF called for Pakistan’s fiscal policy to go beyond achieving primary balance targets and protecting debt sustainability by reinforcing policy credibility, fostering economic recovery and creating space for public investment and social spending to support stronger long-term growth. Encounter culture

Ten suspected criminals killed in Karachi police ‘encounters’ in two days should trouble anyone concerned about public safety and the rule of law. Seven died in separate incidents on Thursday and another three on Friday. In each case, police reported armed resistance, exchanges of fire, and the recovery of weapons.These accounts may be accurate, but cannot be accepted without scrutiny. Officers confronting armed criminals have every right to defend themselves and others. Karachi’s residents, long exposed to street crime and violent robberies, deserve protection. Yet the frequency of these so-called encounters raises questions about how operations are conducted and whether suspects are given an opportunity to surrender. Whether the police actions were legitimate or unlawful can only be established through credible investigations. There must be forensic examination of weapons, independent verification of police accounts, and proper documentation of injuries and evidence. Such scrutiny should be routine whenever police gunfire causes death, regardless of the victim’s alleged criminal history. Otherwise, the public must rely entirely on the version offered by those who pulled the trigger. The concern extends beyond Karachi. In Punjab, where the Crime Control Department faces allegations of staged encounters, the political response has exposed a dangerous willingness to equate police killings with justice. After a suspect in the killing of a teenage worker in Multan died in an alleged encounter, PML-N lawmaker Hina Parvez Butt celebrated it as “justice served immediately”. She deleted the post but defended her remarks as public sentiment. Anger over a child’s murder is understandable. But an elected representative ought to know that public outrage cannot substitute for a trial, and that a police killing is not a judicial verdict. Punjab Information Minister Azma Bokhari criticised the comments, though the issue is larger than one politician’s embarrassment. Governments must make clear that police performance will be judged by crimes prevented, offenders lawfully arrested, and cases successfully prosecuted, not bodies counted. Independent inquiries into disputed encounters should publish findings and establish responsibility if unlawful force is proved. Police officers need training, equipment and supervision that make arrest a realistic option where possible. A force that can shoot but cannot build a case offers little lasting security. Those with high rates of education, mobility, and wealth have both the knowledge of new technologies and the financial bandwidth to adopt them. For Pakistan, this meant that solarisation’s initial home was in the DHAs and Bahria Towns of the country. The residential make-up of these housing societies includes a cross-section of Pakistan’s elite, a central faction of which is the traditional landlord. These are families that have historically owned large swathes of cultivated land, and their incomes primarily come from the rents they accrue from large-scale farming in these rural areas, where they employ labourers, rent land to tenants and still maintain kinship ties. As part of the elite, they were also participants in the first wave of early adopters; many of them bought solar PV systems for their residential properties in Lahore, Multan and other urban centers. Some of these installations were well before solar prices had fallen to the level they have now. Anees Khawaja, a textile business tycoon and landlord based in Multan, first installed a 22-kW system in 2010, when, “it was the costliest solar. So, this is here to stay.” Muhammad Mujahid, CEO of Innovo Corporation (which distributes several major solar panel brands across Pakistan), also identified landlords as the “first movers” with a “herd behaviour” following. What sets these landlords apart from the rest of the affluent class is their spatial fluidity between rural and urban areas. Aided by improved roads and communication infrastructure in Punjab, their economic, social and political bases transcend the urban-rural divide. She has never been more than an hour’s journey away from her village but does not see solar PV (photovoltaic) as an alien technology but a fundamental part of her understanding of life and work. Solar adoption by landlords had a powerful demonstration effect on surrounding poorer communities, who were now exposed to a purportedly transformative technology at the very moment they needed an off-ramp from grid and diesel-based electricity. This exposure came through the most trusted channel possible: a kinship network whose members had already seen the technology work and could vouch for its benefits firsthand. Both information and demonstration effects were thus in play. Farmers heard it was beneficial and saw the proof themselves. Solar distributors are well aware of this phenomenon. The proposed law that seeks amendments to both legal instruments — the Privatisation Commission Ordinance 2000 and Public Private Partnership Authority Act 2017 — also provides legal and investigation immunity to the current and all former chairmen, secretaries, board members, officials and consultants from any act of omission or commission in good faith and in personal capacity and commits public funds to contest any such cases. The all-powerful commission will be led by the incumbent chairman Muhammad Ali and will comprise a majority of members from the private sector, including two women directors. It will collectively administer four public funds — privatisation fund, public contributory fund, viability gap fund and PPP project development funds — created through allocation in the federal budget, receipts from international lenders, income from investments or any other source. The government claims that the PC Ordinance dealt with privatisation of SOEs under the government policy while P3A Act governed a separate body. Both frameworks were concerned with mobilising private sector capital and expertise in relation to public assets, infrastructure and services and their administration through two separate institutions entailed duplication of institutional functions and processes. Therefore, the government’s pol­icy decision was needed for establishing a single institutional framework and mechanism for carrying out privatisation and public private partnership transactions in an efficient manner. For this, it seeks to reconstitute the Privatisation Commission as the Privatisation and Public-Private Partnership Commission, to repeal the Public Private Partnership Authority Act 2017 and dissolve the P3A. The bill provides for the reconstitution of the board with a majority of independent members, including members with public private partnership experience and at least two women, fixed terms of office and clear grounds for disqualification and removal. The bill also empowers the proposed reconstituted commission to conduct functions of advising on, appraising, structuring, facilitating and monitoring public private partnership projects and the establishment of the viability gap fund, the PPP project development facility, and the public contribution fund. The draft claims that it provides a comprehensive legal framework for public private partnerships, including the contractual framework for PPP agreements, a federal PPP pipeline, the approval process, the risk management unit and PPP transaction execution committees. It also empowers the commission to set up PPP nodes within government agencies, settlement of disputes and an alternative process for below-threshold projects. The proposed law enables the commission to utilise public contribution fund to meet any government obligations with respect to PPP projects, including land acquisition, site handover, right of way and any such other enabling actions as may be required in connection with the project. All these funds would be non-lapsable. The PPP agreement between the public and private parties shall be a public document and any interested person shall be entitled to obtain its copy subject to applicable laws, and the government or the commission or the government agency shall not be held liable for any claim of the private party for a risk which is not specified in the PPP agreement. All existing contracts signed by P3A would stand transferred to the new commission without any disadvantage to the private parties. The proposed law empowers the commission to require in writing from any government agency to submit a complete list of its existing, proposed and planned projects, including projects under consideration or development, whether or not identified as PPP projects within fifteen days. The agencies shall be obligated to comply with such a requirement with full accuracy and completeness. Where a government agency fails to comply with any such requirement, the PC chairman may refer the matter to the ministry or division concerned for compliance. There will be a committee to review potential PPP projects proposed for inclusion in the federal PPP pipeline and shall provide its recommendations, which shall be placed before the board for consideration and decision. All officers, consultants, advisers, auditors, employees and other staff of the P3A shall upon the dissolution of the P3A be retained by the commission for a period of six months or until the expiration of their respective contracts, whichever is earlier, on terms and conditions not less favourable than those applicable to them immediately before the dissolution. KP Governor Kundi says Sohail Afridi, assembly speaker 'in safe hands' after house arrest

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi said on Sunday that Sohail Afridi and Assembly Speaker Babar Saleem Swati were “in safe hands” after being placed under house arrest. Afridi, who has become non-functional as the KP chief minister, has not been seen since the Centre imposed governor’s rule and emergency in his province on Oct 11. After their whereabouts remained unknown that day, a federal minister confirmed the next day that he and some of his cabinet members were in preventative custody. However, with no further details officially confirmed, Afridi’s brother and Swati’s cousin have approached the Peshawar High Court separately to seek their relative’s recovery. Appearing on Geo News’ TV programme ‘Jirga’ aired on Sunday night, the governor — who now runs the province — was asked about the whereabouts of Afridi, Kundi said he had been placed under house arrest and was “in safe hands — there is no need to worry”. He said that if there were cases against Afridi, action would be taken against him and he would be indicted. However, if there were no cases, he would be released. Asked why Afridi was not being released if there were no cases against him, Kundi replied he could provide an update tomorrow regarding the matter. The governor further said that Speaker Swati was also under house arrest and would also be freed if there were no charges against him. Responding to another question, Kundi accused the PTI member of committing “illegal and unconstitutional” acts in his role as the KP Assembly Speaker. Asked how many people were self-arrested and how many were “genuinely” arrested, Kundi said he did not have these numbers but that about 40 or 50 per cent of them were “under self-imposed arrest or self-concealment”. The governor also addressed the imposition of emergency and governor’s rule in KP, stressing that it had been the “absolute last option”. Regarding the potential duration of the emergency, he said, “We will see … after six months are complete, we will talk about what will and will not happen. “But in my opinion, maybe it will run longer,” he added, clarifying that this was a “personal opinion” and he could be wrong. He said that the basis for saying this was that the Centre and province needed to be “on the same page” in the fight against terrorism and “streamline” efforts against it, and was making plans accordingly, which he believed would take some time. Kundi also distanced himself and the state policy from purported remarks made by Malik Khan Marjan — a member of the newly formed Area Representative and Development Council (ARDC) for 12 districts of KP — wherein he favoured dialogue with the Afghan Taliban. “I will definitely check on this, but our policy is very clear: for now, we will not be holding talks with Afghanistan,” he maintained. The governor condemned acts of terrorism carried out against Pakistan from Afghan soil and criticised Kabul for “allowing” India to use its soil against Pakistan while also asking for dialogue. “If there is terrorism from there … our army knows how to fix it,” he added. “There is no dialogue and no talks of any kind about this.” “I think this is his (Marjan’s) personal opinion, but it is not our policy,” Kundi said.
