Addressing the forum on Saturday, the minister said the enemy of this civilisation was not another civilisation but climate change, noting that Nepal’s representative also spoke about the issue. Malik recalled the Aug 26 floods in Nepal, which claimed over 1,400 lives and left thousands of others missing, as well as the devastating 2022 floods in Pakistan. He noted that those monsoon floods left 1,700 people dead and submerged one-third of the country, including almost the entire Sindh. “Four days of rain caused Mohenjo Daro’s walls to fall; those walls that no one was able to break for 4,500 years, climate [change] and rain-induced floods did it.” The minister remarked that humans were also an “enemy”, as they contributed to climate destruction. “Do you know what is the meaning of Mohenjo-Daro?” Malik asked. “It means mound of the dead.” Malik said the ancient site had, in a sense, opened its gates to the living during the 2022 floods, providing refuge to people displaced by the disaster. He said people had sought shelter wherever they could find it, including parking lots and shopping malls, while mosques, temples, shrines and schools in Balochistan had also opened their doors to those affected by the floods. “This is the power of civilisation. This is the power through which nations survive,” the minister noted. Digital tax Costa, who represents the EU’s 27 states, is seeking to reach a deal by the end of the year balancing contributions from member states with new sources of revenue for the bloc. Among these could be a tax on digital firms, which the draft Costa presented on Saturday says will concern companies with an annual turnover for digital activities of at least €50 million in the EU as well as a global turnover of more than €750m.
