The Supreme Court bench, which also comprised Justice Naeem Akhtar Afghan, directed the appellant to pay Rs500,000 in costs to the respondent, Sabiha Iqbal, within 30 days of the judgement. Declaring the respondent the rightful owner of the house and the 1994 “gift deed” produced by the appellant legally ineffective, the apex court directed the relevant sub-registrar and revenue officials to make the necessary amendments to the official records. “Love gives; coercion extracts. A hiba is born only of the first. What is born of the second is not a gift but a ransom,” the judgement stated. It said the appeal raised the question of whether a house that a wife was made to sign away as the price of keeping her marriage intact could be considered a gift to her husband. “The answer of Islam, of the statute book and of the conscience of the SC is one and the same: it cannot be called gift,” the judgement explained. The couple married in 1976 and had two children. In 1983, a six-marla plot in Lahore was purchased in Sabiha Iqbal’s name for Rs42,000. She obtained a loan of Rs81,000 from HBFC in 1985 to construct a house. She maintained that she had also contributed towards its construction by selling her jewellery and receiving financial assistance from relatives. She lived in the house with her children. However, a registered gift deed transferred the property to her husband in 1994. Following their separation in August 1998, she discovered the transfer and filed a suit, alleging that her signatures and thumb impressions had been fraudulently obtained on blank stamp paper under the pretext of arranging loan repayments. While rejecting the husband’s claim to the property, Justice Waheed also considered whether the appeal should be dismissed without imposing costs. “Given the circumstances stated above, our answer is no,” the judgement said. The costs, it observed, were not merely the price of losing a case but also served as a balm for the wounds inflicted by a vexatious litigant. “The respondent has been kept in court for over 28 years over the only home she and her children have. She has been forced to defend, at four tiers of the judicial hierarchy, a title that was hers on the day she bought it,” the judge observed. “In exercise of our powers under Article 187 of the Constitution, read with Section 35-A of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 and the Rules of this Court, we consider that exemplary costs are warranted, both as compensation to the respondent and as a warning to others who would use the courts to wear down the women they once promised to protect,” it stated.
