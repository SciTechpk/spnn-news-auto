Govt seeks flat Rs3.04 per unit fuel cost adjustment for Oct-Dec

ISLAMABAD: The government has asked the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (Nepra) to allow a flat additional fuel cost adjustment (FCA) of Rs3.04 per unit for three months — October to December — as an equalised, forward-looking monthly FCA (FFCA) to avoid major price shocks. Acting on the government’s directives, Nepra has called an emergency public hearing for Tuesday (Dec 2) to approve the FCA, which will be applicable from December’s electricity bills. The government had announced last week that the additional FCA would be capped at 83 paisa per unit. When contacted, a Power Division spokesperson said the comparison was with September’s FCA of Rs2.37 per unit. “The ministry has requested an FFCA of Rs3.04 per kWh, applicable for the period from October to December 2026,” Nepra said in its public hearing notice. The ministry’s petition stated that an additional FCA of Rs2.88 per unit had been requested for October over the reference rate already charged to consumers, for which a public hearing had been completed. The additional FCA for November was estimated at 92 paisa per unit, while that for December was estimated at Rs5.13 per unit. The average for the three months was thus calculated at Rs3.04 per unit. The petition said the equalised FFCA had been worked out on the basis of available fuel prices, estimated LNG supplies, additional gas allocation and reliance on thermal fuels. It, therefore, requested Nepra to take up the matter and initiate the necessary proceedings, in accordance with the policy guidelines and applicable framework, to incorporate the FFCA into the existing monthly FCA mechanism, effective from the October 2026 FCA, and determine the applicable FFCA for the period from October to Dec 2026. The federal cabinet approved the changes to the existing monthly FCA mechanism through circulation, issuing policy guidelines to Nepra. The policy guidelines stated that the National Electricity Policy, approved by the Council of Common Interests (CCI) in 2013, provided for the “financial sustainability of the sector premised on the recovery of the full cost of service, to the extent feasible, through an efficient tariff structure, which ensures sufficient liquidity in the sector” through a predictable and uniform framework. The Power Division said the FCA was normally recovered with a two-month lag. An increase in fuel prices and changes in economic parameters resulted in higher fuel charges. Delays in recovering these variations, it added, created a huge liquidity crisis in the power sector. The recent regional conflict has triggered a sharp increase in international fuel prices and disrupted fuel supply chains following the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Consequently, Brent crude oil prices surged above $120 per barrel, compared with the benchmark price of approximately $70 per barrel assumed during the base tariff determination. Amid the force majeure declared by Qatar, Pakistan has continued to manage its re-gasified liquefied natural gas (RLNG) requirements through available contracted supplies and additional spot cargoes, wherever necessary. Similarly, international coal markets have also been affected, resulting in higher coal prices and increased uncertainty in global supply chains. Consequently, the cost of imported coal has risen to $123 per tonne, compared with the benchmark of approximately $85 per tonne assumed during the base tariff determination. This could have implications for the fuel cost of imported coal-fired power generation. Therefore, the flat additional FCA of Rs3.04 per unit has been proposed for three months under the FFCA.
