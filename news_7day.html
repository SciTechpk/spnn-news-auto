All the transactions like Eurobond, Panda bond or private placements have been replacement bonds to replace expensive with cheaper facilities,” he said, adding that fiscal discipline had helped contain the fiscal and current account deficits and, despite oil and wheat import bills, the current account deficit would be no more than 1pc this year. “This is hard-earned stability that helped create buffers to meet our requirements despite floods and war in the region, and foreign exchange reserves crossed $21.5bn, which is the best-ever level of reserves in our history,” he said, adding that Pakistan could not afford to lose this basic hygiene. Finance Secretary Imdadullah Bosal told the committee, which was presided over by Senator Saleem Mandviwalla, that public debt had declined to 68.6pc of GDP by the end of June 2026, from 75.1pc in fiscal year 2022-23. Year-on-year growth in public debt had slowed to 7.7pc in the last fiscal year, the lowest in 20 years, he said. The secretary said domestic debt amounted to Rs58.8 trillion by the end of August 2026, down from Rs68.4tr at the end of June, while foreign debt remained stable at $98.2bn. He reported net financing requirements of $11bn after rollovers and refinancing, of which $5bn had already been repaid in the first four months of the current fiscal year. The remaining $6bn was payable over the next seven months. The committee took strong exception to the difficulties faced by parliamentarians in obtaining foreign exchange for personal travel, saying the humiliation they faced at the hands of banks and the central bank was unacceptable. Mr Mandviwalla directed the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) to review existing procedures, including clearance requirements relating to politically exposed persons (PEPs) and the mandatory personal appearance of parliamentarians before exchange companies, despite their submission of valid passports, tickets and visas. The committee also expressed serious displeasure over the SBP’s response, dated Oct 30, 2026, to a letter sent by the committee secretary on its behalf to the SBP governor. It observed that the matter had been addressed by a deputy director-level officer instead of an appropriately senior authority. The chairman termed the requirement for personal appearance humiliating and called for an immediate review of the existing procedures, warning that the committee would take serious notice if the matter remained unresolved.
