3rd phase of AJK elections: PML-N poised for gains in Poonch amid rigging allegations

The PML-N appeared set to gain at least one seat in Poonch division, where four constituencies went to the polls on Monday amid allegations of rigging, violence and disruption, with polling suspended at some places. The biggest upset was the defeat of Muslim Conference president Sardar Attique Ahmed Khan in LA-14 (Dhirkot), a constituency from where he, and before him his uncle and father, had been elected in all nine elections since 1985. His defeat also appeared to underscore the political decline of what was once the largest and longest-ruling party in Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), but was reduced to a marginal force following the emergence of the PML-N in 2010. Khan was defeated by Sajid Iqbal Abbasi, who contested his first election on the ticket of the little-known Awami Dast-o-Bazu Party, by a margin of more than 21,000 votes, according to unofficial and unverified results from 213 of the 214 polling stations. Observers attributed Abbasi’s victory largely to his hands-on efforts for the development of the constituency and the welfare of its residents, which, they said, had helped him build a personal support base over the years. PML-N’s Raja Faisal Azad and PPP’s Raja Mubashar Ejaz secured 3,000 and 2,800 votes, respectively. In another closely watched contest in LA-17 (Haveli), a neck-and-neck fight was under way between Prime Minister Faisal Mumtaz Rathore and PML-N candidate Engr Chaudhry Mohsin Aziz. Initial reports suggested that Aziz had established a sizeable lead, but it later emerged that results from nearly two dozen polling stations were still awaited. Independent candidate Khawaja Tariq Saeed, who had initially been tipped to finish second, was trailing far behind. In LA-15 (Bagh Central), Jamaat-i-Islami’s Brig (retd) Dr Mohammad Khan was initially reported to be heading for victory, but the contest later narrowed to a two-way race between PML-N’s Mushtaq Ahmed Minhas and PPP’s Sardar Ziaul Qamar. Late-night unofficial and unverified results showed Qamar establishing a clear lead of 2,000 votes over Mr Minhas. Istehkam-i-Pakistan Party (IPP) president and former AJK prime minister Sardar Tanveer Ilyas, who had won the constituency in the 2021 election on a PTI ticket, appeared to be out of the race. In LA-16 (Bagh East), PML-N’s Sardar Mir Akbar was leading PPP’s Sardar Qamaruz Zaman, who is the father of the PPP candidate in LA-15 and has served as an AJK minister in several PPP governments. Akbar had secured 14,900 votes, while Mr Qamar had polled 10,960. Results from 14 polling stations were awaited. Elections are being held across AJK in phases amid tensions between protesters and the authorities. Citing law and order concerns, the Election Commission had decided to postpone polling in Bagh and Sudhnoti districts that was originally set for today. Polling began at 8am and continued until 5pm in four Poonch constituencies. At least 100 killed after powerful earthquake hits western Colombia

At least 100 people were killed after a powerful earthquake struck western Colombia early on Monday, knocking down buildings across several cities and injuring at least 87 more, with still others trapped in the rubble. The quake struck just days after Abelardo De La Espriella was sworn in as president, thrusting the new administration into its first major crisis, with memories still fresh of devastating twin earthquakes in neighboring Venezuela in June. The Colombian geological service and its American counterpart, the US Geological Survey, put the magnitude at 7.4. Colombia had initially put it at 6.6. The quake, according to USGS, struck at a depth of 107km near San Jose del Palmar, in Choco, a sparsely populated province on Colombia’s Pacific Coast, but was felt in major cities nationwide. “Significant casualties and damage are likely and the disaster is potentially widespread,” the agency warned. The highest reported death toll was in the western province of Risaralda in Colombia’s coffee-growing region, where Governor Juan Diego Patino, speaking by phone to Caracol Television, reported 42 fatalities. In the province’s capital of Pereira, social media images showed a building with a cracked facade crash to the ground, sending up a dark cloud of dust and debris as onlookers on the street darted away. The impact also toppled one of the towers atop a Gothic-style cathedral in Manizales, and forced nurses in Cali to wheel patients outside in hospital beds, social media and television images showed. Govt leaves petrol, diesel prices unchanged for Aug 11

The government on Monday kept the prices of petrol and high-speed diesel unchanged as Platts prices were not published on August 10. Petrol will continue to be sold at Rs327.62 per litre, while HSD will cost Rs380.86 per litre. The government continues to levy Rs114 per litre in taxes and duties on petrol and Rs100 per litre on HSD. According to the Petroleum Division’s notification, the new prices will be applicable on August 11. The price of HSD has come down from a peak of Rs520.35 recorded on April 3. Its price had started rising from Rs281 per litre after the US-Iran war broke out on February 28. The petrol price had peaked at Rs458.41 on April 3 after beginning its upward trajectory from Rs266 in the first week of March. Earlier, Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik announced that fuel prices would now be fixed on a daily basis due to fluctuations in international market prices following renewed hostilities between Iran and the US. The government had been announcing weekly revisions to fuel prices since early March, alongside measures for the conservation of fuel amid possible oil supply disruptions due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. The federal government in April also announced targeted relief measures to provide subsidised fuel. The petroleum minister stated that the cabinet and the prime minister had decided to give the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra) the responsibility of deciding fuel prices on a daily basis based on international market trends. The daily pricing decision was rejected by the All Pakistan Dealers Association, which said it would consider a protest plan. Petrol is mainly used in private transport, small vehicles, rickshaws and two-wheelers, and changes in its price affect the middle and lower-middle classes. Similarly, changes in diesel prices also impact the public at large, as it is mainly used in the heavy transport sector, power plants and large generators. Petrol and high-speed diesel (HSD) are the major revenue earners, with monthly sales of about 700,000 to 800,000 tonnes, compared to just 10,000 tonnes of monthly demand for kerosene.

New investigation team initiates formal proceedings into Mir Raza Ali murder

A new investigation team formed by the Sindh IGP on Monday initiated formal proceedings into the murder of business owner Mir Raza Ali, following the findings of a second post-mortem examination. Owner of eatery Wafflix, Ali had gone missing on July 28, and his body was found a day later with a gunshot wound in bushes in Karachi’s Gulistan-i-Jauhar. The deceased’s family, along with their lawyer, Jibran Nasir, met members of the new investigation team at the DIG Crime office. The team also met investigators from the previous team and obtained records related to the case. DIG Crime and Investigation Amir Farooqi, who heads the new team, told Dawn that the investigators heard the family and their lawyer in detail about the circumstances surrounding Ali’s murder. “The police will keep the family informed of all steps being taken to solve the case,” he said. He said the new team had obtained the investigation records from the previous team to review the entire case file. Sindh Home Minister Ziaul Hassan Lanjar said earlier today that the provincial government had deferred the decision on the formation of a judicial commission to probe the Mir Raza Ali murder case. Lanjar made the announcement on X, saying that Karachi Mayor Murtaza Wahab had spoken to Ali’s family, who had expressed “confidence in the new police team” constituted to investigate the case and was of the view that it should conduct the probe. “Therefore, the decision of establishing a judicial commission has been deferred for the time being,” the home minister said. While his family was convinced that he was kidnapped, tortured and murdered, police had previously been building the case around the possibility that he died by suicide. A murder charge was eventually added to the first information report after the exhumation and a second postmortem of Ali’s body revealed that he had suffered multiple injuries and was shot in the back, ruling out the possibility of suicide. Controversy fueled by ‘miscommunication’ Sindh Inspector General of Police (IGP) Javed Alam Odho said that the investigation into the murder should be conducted in a better manner, expressing hope that the new investigation team would consider all available evidence. Speaking to the media in Sukkur, he said the government, public and police were all concerned about the case and stressed the need to examine it “with an open mind and open heart”. He said action had been taken against officers found guilty of misconduct and negligence during the investigation. IG Odho said the controversy surrounding Ali’s murder had been fuelled by “miscommunication”. “I have strictly instructed the entire investigation team to keep communication open and transparent this time, whether with the deceased’s family or the media,” he said. On Sunday, PPP leader Faryal Talpur recommended a judicial commission to probe the case, saying it should examine all aspects of the incident and bring the facts to light. A few hours after Talpur’s statement, Lanjar and Wahab had reached Ali’s residence, met with his father, Mir Hussain Ali, and assured the family that they would be provided complete justice. Talking to the media after the meeting, Lanjar had said a decision had been taken to conduct a judicial inquiry, under the supervision of a Sindh High Court judge, into the murder case. However, Jibran Nasir, the family’s lawyer, posted on X that they had not requested or consented to any judicial commission. “Nor do we believe it’s required at this stage. A new investigation team has been formed, which the family will meet tomorrow. The Sindh government should not try to create hurdles/confusion in the name of ‘facilitation,’“ he said. He had earlier also objected to the initially constituted police team probing the case and demanded that it be replaced. Speaking to the media on Saturday after the results of the second autopsy came out, he had said that he and the family had “no faith” in the investigation team. Subsequently, the Sindh inspector general of police’s office notified on Sunday the reconstitution of the investigating team probing the case. Controversy over autopsy The latest developments in the case follow a controversy between the Karachi police surgeon and investigators over alleged loopholes in the medico-legal report regarding Ali’s first post-mortem examination. The police surgeon noted that entry wounds were typically smaller than exit wounds in firearm cases. She also said that gunshot residue, commonly found on the hands in suspected suicides, was not tested for in this case. Her interview prompted the police authorities to issue a statement, terming such observations as having the potential to adversely affect the ongoing investigation. The statement, however, said that exhumation of the body could be done on the medical board’s recommendation for further clarification by a committee of medico-legal experts with the court’s permission. On August 6, a judicial magistrate had allowed the exhumation of the body on an application filed by Ali’s father, directing the police surgeon and the Sindh health secretary to constitute a medical board for the exhumation. The Sindh health department then constituted an eight-member board, with Dr Syed as the convener. However, in the early hours of August 7, the board was reconstituted through an order issued by the director general of the Sindh health department. While Dr Syed remained the convener of the new five-member board, its members were changed. But Ali’s family rejected the new board, which also resulted in the deferment of the planned exhumation on Friday. Meanwhile, Nasir accused the authorities of trying to bury the facts.
