They can make transactions easier to trace, reduce delays and help bring economic activity into the formal system. Raast and other payment infrastructure can make this possible at a scale that cash cannot. But encouraging digital payments is different from withdrawing cash before people have a workable alternative. That distinction matters. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics’ Household Integrated Economic Survey 2024-25 puts the gap in perspective. Internet use among individuals stands at 57 per cent, falling to 49pc in rural areas, compared with 69pc in urban areas. Only half the population owns a mobile phone. These figures do not tell us how many people can use a banking application, but they do show how uneven access to the basic tools of a cashless economy remains.
