He argued that the polls, scheduled for Dec 20, could not be held until the legal status of the existing constituency delimitation was conclusively determined. A single-member bench comprising Justice Umair Majeed Malik granted the interim relief while hearing the writ petition on Friday. “Operation of the impugned order dated Oct 21, 2026, and the impugned election schedule dated Oct 21, 2026, as revised by the Election Commission, shall remain suspended until the next date of hearing,” the court order read. The court also issued notices to the respondents, directing them to submit their reports and para-wise comments before the next hearing, scheduled for Nov 26, 2026. Advocate Barrister Qazi Adil Aziz appeared for the petitioner, along with Malik Talha and Muhammad Yaseen Iqbal. They argued that under Articles 140-A(2), 218(3) and 219(d) of the Constitution, the ECP was “duty-bound to hold local government elections honestly, justly, fairly and in accordance with the law”. The counsel contended that the election programme had become legally compromised due to legislative changes between the original Islamabad Capital Territory Local Government Act, 2015 (LG Act 2015), and the subsequent Islamabad Capital Territory Local Government Ordinance, 2026 (LG Ordinance 2026). According to the petition, the LG Act 2015 provided for one Metropolitan Corporation and 125 Union Councils, each divided into nine wards. The delimitation process for this structure began in December 2024, and an election programme was issued on Dec 26, 2025, but was subsequently withdrawn. Meanwhile, the LG Ordinance 2026 altered the capital’s local government structure by introducing three Town Corporations, changing the process for constituting and delimiting Union Councils, removing the nine-ward structure and providing for delimitation on the basis of census blocks. Consequently, the number of Union Councils increased from 125 to 130. The counsel submitted that the ECP subsequently issued revised delimitation timelines on Aug 25, 2026, and completed the process with the publication of the final list of 130 Union Councils on Oct 14, 2026. However, after a single 120-day extension, the LG Ordinance 2026 ceased to have effect under Article 89 of the Constitution, resulting in the revival of the original LG Act 2015. At this point, the counsel argued, the ECP suddenly issued the impugned order and election schedule to conduct polls under the original LG Act 2015. The petitioner’s counsel contended that the impugned order did not address the delimitation of the 125 Union Councils, their nine wards or the Metropolitan Corporation under the revived law. They argued that the delimitation carried out under the amended law could not serve as the basis for elections under the revived LG Act 2015. They therefore maintained that fresh delimitation and preparation of electoral rolls in accordance with the LG Act 2015 and the Elections Act, 2017, were necessary before the elections could lawfully be held. The counsel further argued that holding elections without completing the required delimitation would violate Article 218(3) of the Constitution and the electoral framework under the LG Act 2015 and the Elections Act, besides resulting in unnecessary expenditure of public funds. After hearing the initial arguments, the court observed that the points raised required consideration and suspended the operation of the ECP’s order and election schedule until the next hearing, scheduled for Nov 26.
