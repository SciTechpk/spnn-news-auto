Thus, when Tudeh resurfaced in the lead-up to the 1979 revolution, it wasn’t starting from scratch. It participated in the mass surge against the tyrannical Shah, but, unlike the militant Mujahideen-i-Khalq and others on the left, acknowledged the Khomeini phenomenon as a legitimate expression of the popular will. That helps to explain why Tudeh briefly became the main opposition in a milieu dominated by the Islamic Republican Party.
