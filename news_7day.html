Constitutional provisions Emergency Article 232 of the Constitution provides for the proclamation of emergency in a province. It says, “If the president is satisfied that a grave emergency exists in which the security of Pakistan, or any part thereof, is threatened by war or external aggression, or by internal disturbance beyond the power of a provincial government to control, he may issue a Proclamation of Emergency.” However, invoking this article “due to internal disturbances beyond the powers of a provincial government to control” requires a resolution from the provincial assembly of that province. In case the president “acts on his own” — which is not currently the case — the Proclamation of Emergency is to be placed before both houses of Parliament for approval by each house within 10 days. According to this provision, parliament “shall have power to make laws for a province, or any part thereof, with respect to any matter not enumerated in the Federal Legislative List” during the time of the proclamation of an emergency. Nothing in this Article shall restrict the power of a provincial assembly to make any law which the Constitution empowers it to make, but if any provision of a provincial law is repugnant to any provision of an Act by Parliament, the latter would prevail and the provincial law would be void as long as the parliamentary Act is in effect. Governor’s rule On the other hand, Article 234 deals with the imposition of the governor’s rule “in case of failure of constitutional machinery in a province”. For imposition of the governor’s rule, the president simply requires a request from the provincial governor, it says. “A proclamation issued under this Article shall be laid before a joint sitting and shall cease to be in force at the expiration of two months, unless before the expiration of that period it has been approved by resolution of the joint sitting and may by like resolution be extended for a further period not exceeding two months at a time,” the Constitution reads. However, it adds that no such proclamation shall in any case remain in force for more than six months. Article 233, which provides powers to the president to suspend certain fundamental rights during the emergency period, was not mentioned in the summary signed by President Zardari. This isn’t the first time the Centre has used its powers to declare emergency in a province or even send the respective provincial government packing. Here’s a brief history of emergency rule in Pakistan. January 1949 to April 1951 The first governor’s rule was imposed within a short span of the country’s establishment, in the heartland of Punjab. In January 1949, then Prime Minister Liaquat Ali Khan visited Lahore, where Chief Minister Nawab Mamdot told him that he had earned the confidence of a majority of the MPAs in the provincial assembly. The development had come about due to a disagreement between Mamdot and former CM Mumtaz Daulatana. However, Liaquat Ali Khan noted factual discrepancies in the document shown by Mamdot. He dismissed the Mamdot government, dissolved the assembly, and imposed governor’s rule in the province. December 1951 to May 1953 — Sindh A governor’s rule of 1 year and 144 days lasted in Sindh after Chief Minister Muhammad Ayub Khuhro was dismissed amid allegations of corruption and maladministration. Mian Aminuddin administered the province directly under Governor’s rule, which ended following the 1953 provincial elections, after which Pirzada Abdus Sattar of the Pakistan Muslim League took over. February-April, 1973 — Balochistan On February 14, 1973, Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto dismissed the Balochistan provincial government led by Chief Minister Sardar Attaullah Mengal and removed Governor Mir Ghaus Bakhsh Bizenjo, citing alleged involvement in unrest and the discovery of weapons at the Iraqi embassy in Islamabad. The next day, Nawab Akbar Khan Bugti was appointed as the new Governor of Balochistan, and direct central/governor’s rule was enforced. The federal government also subsequently deployed the Pakistan Army under General Tikka Khan to conduct operations in the mountainous and tribal regions, particularly targeting areas like Lasbela and Marri. The governor’s rule ended in late April the same year, paving the way for Jam Ghulam Qadir Khan to form a new provincial ministry. February-May 1975 — Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor’s rule was imposed in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (then known as the North-West Frontier Province) from February 16, 1975, to May 3, 1975, for a total duration of 76 days under Article 232 of the 1973 Constitution due to an emergency. Officials had cited external disruptions beyond local control. At the time, the federal government under PM Bhutto had directed the governor to assume all government powers, ending the cabinet of Chief Minister Sardar Inayatullah Khan Gandapur. The province was overseen by Major General (R) Syed Ghawas until direct rule concluded on May 3, 1975, with Nasrullah Khan Khattak assuming the office of Chief Minister. June-August 1988 — Sindh The second instance of a governor’s rule in Sindh’s history lasted 68 days, from June 24, 1988, to August 31, 1988. The caretaker government of Chief Minister Akhtar Ali Ghulam Qazi of the Islami Jamhoori Ittehad was dismissed. Qazi was replaced by Pakistan Army’s four-star general Rahimuddin Khan as governor.
