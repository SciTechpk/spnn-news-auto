All foreign media personnel to require NOC from info ministry for news coverage outside Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi

All foreign media personnel will now require a no-objection certificate (NOC) from the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (MOIB) to travel to any city outside Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi for news coverage, according to recently issued guidelines. The new Foreign Media Facilitation Guidelines, 2026, outline requirements for the registration and accreditation of “foreign media houses, organisations and outlets working in Pakistan and all eligible journalists, media owners, media professionals, sources, freelancers, sponsors, fixers, attached persons, assignees etc, of Pakistan working or contributing for them”. All foreign media personnel already accredited or registered with the MOIB’s External Publicity (EP) Wing will require a mandatory NOC from the wing for movement to any city except Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi, in order to produce “news content, story, video, film, documentary or social media content” for official use. For leisure trips to Abbottabad and Murree, a NOC may be granted by the EP Wing upon the request of the journalist, within three working days, the notification added. The guidelines apply to all foreign or international print, electronic and digital media, including social media and web-based platforms. According to the new guidelines, all journalists working with international or foreign media outlets must register with the EP Wing through a designated Online Registration and Accreditation portal available at www.dml.nimar.pk/www.registration.epwing.gov.pk. “All Pakistani journalists residing abroad, contributing to foreign media and seeking registration must submit their application through the EP Wing portal and concurrently forward their case through the nearest press attaché, counsellor, minister posted in Pakistan missions, consulates, high commissions, or embassies,” the MOIB said. Additionally, all companies, entities, production houses etc. operating in Pakistan to facilitate foreign media “in any capacity” are also required to register with the ministry’s publicity wing, as are non-journalists living in Pakistan who render services to foreign media. “Registration shall not confer any official status and only establishes professional recognition,” the notification pointed out, adding that registration would only be considered if supported by a recommendation from the head of the organisation. After registration, journalists seeking a press accreditation card must submit their request via the portal alongside required documents. There are two types of accreditation: permanent accreditation to be granted on an annual basis, and temporary accreditation for visiting journalists, valid for a maximum of three months. Upon issuance of the EP Wing’s accreditation card, the wing will “facilitate” the journalists’ access to all official media events and government premises. Journalists will be “bound to produce that card on demand” and the card must be surrendered upon change of category or organisation, or upon the completion of the accreditation tenure. The notification said that registration cards may be issued within seven working days from the submission of an application, while the accreditation card may take four to six weeks. The MOIB may seek additional information and documents if required, it added.

The petrol price had peaked at Rs458.41 on April 3 after beginning its upward trajectory from Rs266 in the first week of March. The government had been announcing weekly revisions to fuel prices since early March, alongside measures for the conservation of fuel amid possible oil supply disruptions due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. The federal government in April also announced targeted relief measures to provide subsidised fuel. The petroleum minister stated that the cabinet and the prime minister had decided to give the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra) the responsibility of deciding fuel prices on a daily basis based on international market trends. The daily pricing decision was rejected by the All Pakistan Dealers Association, which said it would consider a protest plan. Petrol is mainly used in private transport, small vehicles, rickshaws and two-wheelers, and changes in its price affect the middle and lower-middle classes. They said the transport sector was facing an unprecedented financial crisis due to repeated increases in diesel prices, higher taxes and other government measures. The transporters demanded that taxes on diesel be reduced and fuel prices be restored to their July 1, 2024, level. Demands were also issued for a reduction in the withholding tax on cargo transporters from seven per cent to 2pc, arguing that oil tanker operators were already paying only 2pc. The alliance also rejected a provision introduced in the Finance Act 2026 under which an entire vehicle could be confiscated if non-duty-paid goods were found on board. Describing the measure as unjust and demanding its immediate withdrawal, the transporters urged the government to restore the previous system under which fines were imposed and vehicles released. They also sought uniform implementation of axle-load regulations across the country, saying overloading should be checked at factories, warehouses and loading points rather than through roadside enforcement, which they alleged had become a source of revenue collection. Among other demands, the transporters called for a simpler procedure for obtaining heavy transport vehicle driving licences, a reduction in toll taxes and an end to the daily adjustment of diesel prices. They demanded that fuel prices be revised fortnightly or monthly to provide greater certainty for transport operators. All Pakistan Goods Transport Owners Association President Muhammad Owais Chaudhry said the current policies had pushed drivers, small transporters and vehicle owners paying instalments on financed trucks into severe financial hardship, making it increasingly difficult for them to continue operations. “We have repeatedly approached the government through letters, meetings and media appeals, but no meaningful progress has been made,” he said. It was jointly announced that unless the government entered into immediate negotiations and accepted their demands, all categories of goods transport, including cargo trucks, containers and oil tankers, would remain off the roads from Aug 8. The alliance leaders said the strike would remain peaceful, adding that transporters would park their vehicles instead of blocking highways.
