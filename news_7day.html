ECP sets Feb 7 as polling date for first phase of Punjab local govt elections; KP polls delayed

ISLAMABAD: The Election Commission of Pakistan (ECP) on Thursday decided to hold the first phase of local government elections in Punjab on Feb 7, 2027, in view of fog and weather conditions, while polls in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa were postponed until further orders. ECP had initially proposed holding the elections on December 14 in Punjab. In KP, the polls were to be held across the province, excluding 12 districts where special emergency measures were in place. Thursday’s decision was taken by a four-member bench headed by Chief Election Commissioner Sikandar Sultan Raja and comprising members Nisar Ahmed Durrani, Shah Muhammad Jatoi and Babar Hassan Bharwana, during a hearing on the conduct of local government elections in Punjab, according to a statement issued by the ECP. Punjab chief secretary, Minister for Local Government and Community Development Zeeshan Rafique, and the department’s secretary appeared before the commission. At the outset, the chief secretary apprised the commission that an earlier committee constituted by the Punjab government had held a meeting with the ECP, during which it was discussed and mutually decided that the elections would be conducted during the period from December to January. He submitted that the provincial government was fully prepared to conduct elections in the province and that all necessary arrangements had been completed. The requisite information and documents sought by the ECP regarding the appointment of returning officers and district returning officers had already been furnished, and no administrative impediments remained to holding the elections. He stated that the provincial government had no objection to the announcement of the election schedule. The chief secretary, however, requested that polling dates preferably be fixed after Jan 20, 2027, with elections held in phases, keeping in view weather conditions and administrative convenience to ensure the smooth, transparent and orderly conduct of the electoral process. He explained that December and January were generally marked by dense fog in Punjab, particularly in Lahore, which could cause difficulties with transportation, movement and other election-related activities. He also highlighted seasonal conditions and the importance of adequate daylight for the proper conduct of polling. He further informed the commission that the provincial government would be required to arrange transportation facilities and had also made arrangements for the availability of school buildings to be used as polling stations. He said the administration needed an additional two months to complete the necessary arrangements and that conditions generally improved after Jan 20. An ECP member pointed out that the provincial government had previously proposed holding elections in April. Responding to the observation, the chief secretary said the government had reconsidered its position and had no objection to holding elections in January or February. However, he maintained that April would be more suitable because of longer daylight hours and school holidays. He argued that shorter days and cold weather during winter could discourage voters from turning out to cast their ballots, while April would offer more favourable conditions. The commission observed that the ECP was constitutionally obligated under Articles 140A and 219(d) of the Constitution, read with Section 219 of the Elections Act, 2017, to hold local government elections in the provinces, the Islamabad Capital Territory and the cantonment boards. It also noted that it is the duty of the commission under Article 218(3) of the Constitution to organise and conduct the election and to make such arrangements as are necessary to ensure that the election is conducted honestly, justly, fairly and in accordance with law, and that corrupt practices are guarded against. Keeping in view the constitutional provisions and to provide a level playing field to the contesting candidates and voters of the constituencies, the request made by the chief secretary was accepted in the interest of justice. It was further decided that the first phase of local government elections in Punjab will be held with the polling date set as Feb 7, 2027. The office was directed to take follow-up action accordingly. The last local government elections in Punjab were held in 2015 under the Punjab Local Government Act, 2013. Polling took place in phases between Oct and Dec 2015, and elected councils assumed office in 2017 after indirect elections for mayors and chairpersons. Since then, local body polls in Punjab have experienced repeated delays. The PTI government had dissolved the elected local bodies in April 2019 before they completed their tenure. The move was later struck down by the Supreme Court, which restored them. They eventually completed their term on Dec 31, 2021. Successive provincial governments repeatedly amended or replaced the Punjab Local Government Act. Each legislative change required the ECP to restart preparations, including delimitation and election planning. Changing laws and administrative delays by successive governments contributed significantly to the postponement of the local body elections. Under Article 140A of the Constitution and the Elections Act, local government elections should be held within 120 days of the expiry of local bodies’ terms. Since the councils’ term ended in Dec 2021, elections were due by April 2022, but they have been repeatedly postponed. Local govt polls halted in KP Meanwhile, in KP, the ECP halted all preparations for local government elections until further orders after the federal government extended the suspension of Article 140-A of the Constitution to the entire province a day earlier, a statement issued by the commission said. On Oct 28, ECP had decided to hold local government elections in KP, excluding 12 districts which were deemed to face intense security challenges and an Area Representative and Development Council (ARDC) was formed to oversee them under emergency measures. The council was formed after the federal government imposed an emergency in KP on Oct 11 and placed the province under governor’s rule, citing the “provincial government’s deliberate disengagement from the fight against terrorism”. The matter came up for discussion at a meeting of the commission, which was chaired by the chief election commissioner. The commission took up Order No 3, issued by the federal government on Nov 4, 2026, regarding the suspension of Article 140-A. The meeting examined Clause 2 of the order in detail, under which Article 140-A of the Constitution had been suspended and its application extended to all districts other than those covered by the previous order, the statement said. During the briefing, the commission was informed that Article 140-A of the Constitution relates to the establishment of local governments in the provinces, while under sub-clause (2) of the article, holding local government elections is the constitutional responsibility of the ECP. The commission also reviewed in detail the possible impact of suspending Article 140-A in the remaining districts on the ongoing local government electoral process in KP. After deliberation, it was decided that in view of the said order, holding local government elections in the remaining districts of KP was no longer possible. Therefore, all steps taken so far for holding these elections have been stopped until further orders.

The bench took up a set of petitions challenging interim bail of the detainees, granted by the PHC against surety bonds of Rs100,000 each. The PHC had issued the interim orders in the absence of any report by the provincial government explaining reasons and circumstances that led to the detention of PTI activists and also directed that they should not be arrested pending the cases related to their detentions before the high court. The PHC will next hear the case on Nov 12. More than 60 appeals challenging the PHC’s interim orders have been fixed before the FCC. The pleas were moved by the KP Home and Tribal Affairs Department additional chief secretary. The FCC restrained the high court from proceeding further on the cases and decided to issue notices to the parties with a direction that they may also file their pleas before the superior court. KP Advocate General Sanaullah Khan, who pleaded the case before the FCC on behalf of the provincial government, told Dawn that the PHC’s orders had been challenged under Article 245 of the Constitution, which provides for calling in armed forces in aid of civil forces. He cited clause 3 of the provision, arguing that a high court could not “directly entertain a case under Article 199 of the Constitution since it cannot exercise its jurisdiction in relation to any area in which the armed forces are acting in aid of the civil power”. Clause 3 of Article 245 states: “A high court shall not exercise jurisdiction under Article 199 in relation to any area in which the armed forces of Pakistan are, for the time being, acting in aid of civil power.” When the KP advocate general presented this argument before the FCC, Justice Farooq observed that a high court received several cases under Article 199 on a daily basis. “Does this mean the high court should not hear such cases?” he questioned. The advocate general replied that matters such as tenancy disputes could be taken up as the high court was approached for these cases after the matter was referred to competent forums. Former additional advocate general Shah Faisal Ilyas, who was representing one of the parties, raised an objection during the proceedings and requested the court to hear the parties before issuing any injunctive order, asserting that the objections raised by the provincial government against the high court’s order were untenable. CJ Khan then directed the counsel to furnish his formal objections after going through the FCC’s order first and stated that the court was issuing notices to the respondents. The KP advocate general said while speaking to Dawn that the framers of the Constitution had provided protection to provisions relating to the preventive custody under Article 10 (safeguards as to arrest and detention). He specifically cited clauses 3 and 4 of the provision and contended that provisions of the MPO were invoked on the basis of “subjective analysis of the government functionary to prevent any action prejudicial to the public safety and law and order”. The FCC’s directive comes less than a month after the federal government imposed emergency and governor’s rule in KP on Oct 11, citing the provincial government’s “deliberate disengagement” from the fight against terrorism. The move has drawn criticism from the opposition, including Jamiat Ulema-i-Islam-Fazl chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman, National Assembly Opposition Leader Mehmood Khan Achakzai and Senate Opposition Leader Raja Nasir Abbas. In the days preceding the imposition of governor’s rule in KP, political tensions between the Centre and the opposition — including the PTI which was in power in KP — had remained high since jailed former prime minister Imran Khan’s party announced a protest march to Islamabad to demand his release. Intense negotiations between the two sides a week before the imposition of governor’s rule had failed to yield any results. Scores of PTI workers and leaders had been detained following the Oct 11 move, including lawmakers. Sohail Afridi, who has been rendered non-functional as the chief minister after the imposition of governor’s rule, has also been detained. The PHC remains seized with petitions against these detentions, including that of Afridi.
