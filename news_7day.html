Govt transfers ISI’s internal wing chief Maj Gen Faisal Naseer to Nacta

ISLAMABAD: The government, in a surprise move on Friday, transferred Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) Director General (C) Major General Faisal Naseer and appointed him National Coordinator of the National Counter-Terrorism Authority (Nacta). The DG (C) post is often mistakenly described as heading ISI’s counter-intelligence wing. The office actually heads the agency’s Internal Wing, which deals with domestic security and other matters and, because of the nature of the assignment, frequently engages with the country’s principal power centres. Within the ISI, DG (C) is considered virtually the second most important position after that of the ISI director general. Maj Gen Naseer’s transfer is being seen as particularly significant because it comes ahead of the upcoming promotions in the army. An officer from the Corps of Military Intelligence, he was considered a strong candidate for elevation to the rank of lieutenant general. He served as DG (C) from 2022, shortly after his promotion to the rank of major general, and remained in the position through a politically turbulent period. Although there have been exceptions over the years, a transfer to a non-military assignment may affect an officer’s career progression, hence the surprise among many observers of military affairs. Nacta, established in 2009 and subsequently given statutory backing under the Nacta Act of 2013, was conceived as the country’s central civilian body for coordinating counter-terrorism and counter-extremism policies among federal and provincial agencies. In practice, however, the authority has never fully evolved into the command and coordination body envisaged under the law and has largely remained a policy secretariat with limited capacity to enforce decisions across the country’s sprawling security and law enforcement apparatus. The authority has also seen frequent changes at the top, with more than a dozen National Coordinators since its inception, most of them drawn from the police service. Maj Gen Naseer’s appointment therefore marks a departure from the usual practice and could potentially signal an attempt to give greater weight to an institution that has long struggled to establish itself as the central coordinating mechanism for the fight against terrorism and violent extremism. The government did not immediately explain the rationale for the change.

UN adopts major map reform to show true scale of continents

The United Nations General Assembly voted on Friday for a resolution spearheaded by several African countries to adopt a map that makes their continent bigger while shrinking North America and Europe — aiming at “a more accurate representation” of world geography. The resolution was backed by 164 countries while the United States voted against it and six other states abstained. The new map — known as the Equal Earth cartographic projection — “offers a more accurate representation of the sizes of continental landmasses and to this end is a relevant option for improving the accuracy of the cartographic representation of the world,” the resolution says. It urges member states to adopt the new map in place of the traditional Mercator projection, which was designed for maritime navigation by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator in 1569. The foreign minister of Togo, which drove the resolution, said maps “shape our understanding of the world.” “They guide education, nourish the imagination, and influence collective perceptions. They are therefore never neutral, when they present a distorted image of our planet, they risk perpetuating inaccurate representation that are passed down through generation,” Robert Dussey said at the UN. ‘Distorted representation’ The Mercator projection focused on accurate depictions of the shapes and angles of land masses, but their relative sizes were often inaccurate — in part due to the challenge of rendering a three-dimensional globe onto a two-dimensional map. It inflated northern regions and compressed equatorial ones, making Europe and North America appear much larger, while shrinking Africa and South America. A group of cartographers launched the Equal Earth projection in 2018. It makes Africa, Latin America, South Asia and Oceania appear vastly larger. “Equal Earth preserves the relative surface areas of continents and, as much as possible, shows their shapes as they appear on a globe,” one of the cartographers, Bojan Savric, told AFP in 2025. The foreign ministry of France — which co-sponsored the resolution in support of the new map — announced Friday that it would abandon the Mercator projection. In that map, “the United States, Russia and China take on a disproportionate visual prominence compared to Africa, Latin America or southeast Asia,” while “Greenland appears almost as big as Africa,” Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said. “Changing maps obviously doesn’t change the world,” he acknowledged. “But correcting a distorted representation is already an act of truth.” The initiative comes as France is seeking to improve relations with Africa, particularly in former colonies with which it has prickly ties.

Nepra allows govt to mandate Battery Energy Storage Systems for wind, solar IPPs in upcoming power auction

ISLAMABAD: The National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (Nepra) on Friday allowed the government to require mandatory Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) as part of an upcoming auction for 800 megawatts of renewable power capacity from wind and solar projects, aimed at helping stabilise an electricity supply facing serious duck curve challenges. After a delay of more than 30 years, the government has announced the auction process for a notional 800MW of capacity as part of its efforts to gradually move towards a competitive energy market. It is currently engaging with the private sector to finalise the bidding schedule. Facing serious duck curve challenges, the government had decided in July to make BESS mandatory for bidders as part of the upcoming auction. The regulator approved the move through amendments to the “wheeling auction process”, allowing the Independent System Operator (Ismo) — a subsidiary of the power division — to formally issue a request for proposals for the first block of the 400MW auction within the current month. Under the first of three key amendments to the previous wheeling auction process, prospective bidders would be required to commit to having BESS comprise at least 10 per cent of their proposed wind or solar capacity, the regulator stated. “An auction participant that intends to participate in the auction by utilising solar and/or wind generation technologies shall only be eligible to participate in the auction if the firm capacity of its BESS located at the site of the generation facility is equivalent to at least 10pc of the firm capacity of its solar and/or wind generation facility,” it said. For the first auction conducted by Ismo, participants are to submit their proposals within two months of the publication of the request for proposal (RFP). “This period shall be fixed and non-extendable. For all subsequent auctions, auction participants shall submit their proposals within one month of the publication of the RFP,” Nepra said. The rationale for this provision, apart from it being a standard feature of many procurement and auction frameworks, is to give Ismo flexibility during the implementation of the first auction, as a significant number of prospective participants may seek an extension of the submission deadline. The amendments also provide for the constitution of a three-member grievance redressal committee, headed by the managing director of the Private Power and Infrastructure Board (PPIB) and comprising two independent directors of the Ismo board, to address complaints or grievances regarding the auction process. Ismo’s extensive modelling of BESS and its financial implications also indicated that returns from installing BESS increased up to a certain capacity. Based on feedback from participants, a minimum BESS capacity of 10pc has been finalised as a mandatory requirement. However, auction participants may install more than the minimum to further improve their financial viability, as the financial model showed higher returns with greater BESS capacity. According to Ismo, international markets had adopted two approaches to integrating BESS into the grid. Countries with mature markets generally published all relevant information and allowed market players to make appropriate decisions, while developing countries adopted mandatory requirements for BESS integration. Developing markets such as India, the Philippines, China and the Dominican Republic have also implemented mandatory BESS requirements, it said. Most prospective auction participants and other stakeholders, according to Ismo, acknowledged the importance of BESS in addressing grid flexibility and mitigating the challenge of the duck curve caused by renewable energy — for example, large numbers of solar consumers exiting the grid during the day and suddenly returning in the evening, creating a surge in demand. Under the current mechanism, the auction committee is responsible for evaluating eligibility as well as addressing grievances over these evaluations. The private sector, however, called for an independent forum, separate from the auction committee, to address grievances relating to auctions, in order to eliminate an apparent conflict of interest and enhance the credibility and transparency of the process.
