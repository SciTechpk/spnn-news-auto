Alongside SpaceX and its Starmind constellation, Google is pursuing its own initiative under the codename “Suncatcher,” while Jeff Bezos’s Blue Origin has submitted an application to the FCC for its “Project Sunrise.” Several startups are also in the race, including Starcloud, which successfully operated an advanced processor known as a GPU aboard a satellite in late 2025. Google also reached that milestone in October. SpaceX has the advantage of having its own rockets that have already completed hundreds of missions. In August, Musk promised a first SpaceX satellite launch into orbit in the fourth quarter of 2027 and a ramp-up in 2028. The company has struck a deal with Nvidia, which will supply its latest-generation Vera Rubin processors, specially designed to operate in space. ‘Disheartened and dismayed’ Most space-based data centre projects have a similar architecture: a constellation of satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO), positioned close together to ensure reliable connections between them. Starmind’s satellites will communicate with those already in orbit as part of SpaceX’s Starlink satellite internet network, which will then relay the data to Earth. However, there are numerous advantages to locating a data centre in space over building one on Earth. Among those, a satellite in what is known as a sun-synchronous orbit can capture solar energy continuously and with eight times the efficiency of its ground-based equivalent.
