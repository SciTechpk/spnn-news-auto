ISLAMABAD: Pakistan is among the 10 countries bearing the brunt of the global hunger crisis, with climate-related disasters, economic hardship, displacement and shrinking humanitarian funding pushing vulnerable communities deeper into food insecurity, according to a new report released by international humanitarian organisation Action Against Hunger. The report, Ten Largest Global Food Crises, released on Friday in connection with World Food Day, warns that an estimated 10.6 million people in Pakistan will require humanitarian assistance in 2026, despite ongoing recovery efforts and improvements in some macroeconomic indicators. Globally, approximately 177 million people live in the 10 countries where hunger is most concentrated, while acute hunger has doubled over the past decade, affecting 266 million people across 47 countries, says the report. The countries facing the largest food crises are Nigeria, Congo, Sudan, Yemen, Afghan­istan, Myanmar, Bangladesh, Pakistan, South Sudan and Syria. Report sees 10.6m Pakistanis needing humanitarian assistance in 2026 In Pakistan, persistent poverty, high food inflation, rising living costs, limited employment opportunities and declining household incomes continue to erode families’ purchasing power. Unfavourable agricultural prices and broader economic instability have further undermined livelihoods, leaving millions struggling to afford adequate food. As humanitarian needs outstrip available resources, vulnerable households are increasingly resorting to damaging coping strategies, including cutting food consumption, selling productive assets and withdrawing children from school to cope with financial pressures. The report cautions that these measures may provide temporary relief but can deepen poverty, undermine children’s education and nutrition, and weaken families’ ability to recover from future shocks. Limited access to healthcare, nutrition services, clean water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and social protection is compounding the crisis, particularly in rural and disaster-affected areas. Persistent poverty and acute malnutrition remain major drivers of humanitarian needs, while cuts to support services and livelihood programmes are further eroding community resilience. Pakistan’s exposure to climate-related disasters remains a major source of food insecurity. Recurrent floods, droughts, heatwaves, glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs) and increasingly erratic monsoon rainfall have damaged infrastructure, reduced agricultural production and weakened livestock-dependent livelihoods. Crop losses and prolonged dry spells have also increased households’ reliance on markets for food at a time when many families have limited purchasing power. Several districts are experiencing prolonged drought conditions, adding to concerns about agricultural output and rural incomes. The report warns that seasonal hardships can intensify these pressures. The lean season from December 2025 to March 2026, characterised in some areas by extended winter conditions and heavy snowfall, was identified as a period likely to worsen food insecurity by restricting access to markets and essential resources. SC questions ‘lofty expectations’ in criminal cases

ISLAMABAD: The Supreme Court has taken exception to a prevalent practice in criminal trials which, it said, defied normal human capability and natural perception, and observed that eyewitness accounts should reflect genuine human observation rather than physically impossible details concerning firearm incidents. “To expect mathematical precision regarding the number of firearm shots or the exact location of injuries from eyewitnesses exposed to a horrific event is both unnatural and impractical,” observed Justice Ishtiaq Ibrahim in a judgement he authored. Justice Ibrahim was a member of a three-judge SC bench headed by Justice Muhammad Hashim Khan Kakar, which took up criminal appeals filed by Muhammad Azhar and the state challenging a Sept 13, 2021, Lahore High Court, Bahawalpur, ruling. The SC, however, rejected the criminal appeal in the 2017 murder case, upholding Muhammad Azhar’s conviction for killing Sakhian Bibi. Expecting mathematical precision from eyewitnesses exposed to traumatic events is ‘unnatural and impractical’, Justice Ibrahim holds “In criminal jurisprudence, human behaviour should not be subjected to a fixed formula, nor should every witness be expected to react to, observe or retain events in an identical manner,” the judgement said. The incident occurred on Jan 11, 2017, when the appellant allegedly entered the victim’s home armed with a .30-bore pistol, seeking the complainant’s uncle in connection with ongoing civil litigation. When the victim said her uncle was not present, the petitioner opened fire, discharging five shots that struck her chest, abdomen and right arm. The additional sessions judge, Minch­inabad, convicted the petitioner and sentenced him to death, with Rs200,000 in compensation payable to the legal heirs. The LHC, Bahawalpur, later commuted the death sentence to life imprisonment, prompting both the petitioner’s appeal and the state’s petition for enhancement of the sentence. “We frequently observe prosecution witnesses deposing with microscopic precision, as if watching events in slow motion, describing the exact trajectory and anatomical landing site of each individual bullet discharged from a firearm,” the judgement observed. It is humanly impossible for an eyewitness exposed to the sudden terror and high stress of a deadly attack to track a high-velocity bullet in mid-air and confirm precisely where each shot strikes the victim’s body, it noted. The judge emphasised that every individual reacts differently to a sudden traumatic situation: while one person may observe details, another may freeze or lose awareness of the occurrence. In a sudden traumatic event, when multiple gunshots ring out within a few seconds and a mother is murdered in cold blood before her son’s eyes, a minor divergence regarding the exact number of firearm shots or the precise entry point of each bullet is evidence of natural and reasonable human limitations, he observed. It is common knowledge that most individuals are not frequently exposed to dangerous, life-threatening situations, and the sudden brandishing of a weapon induces an atmosphere of shock and fear, according to the judgement. In such stressful encounters, human cognitive and perceptual capacities are severely impaired, the judgement said. To substantiate this point, it cited renowned cognitive psychologist Elizabeth Loftus, who had stated that a witness confronted by an assailant brandishing a firearm involuntarily fixes their attention almost exclusively on the weapon itself. This cognitive phenomenon, known as “attentional tunnelling”, limits the observer’s capacity to process or recall surrounding details, the judgement said. Trial courts should bear these limitations in mind when scrutinising eyewitness accounts claiming microscopic accuracy despite the stressful circumstances, Justice Ibrahim observed. The judgement expected lower courts to assess witnesses in the light of the circumstances of each case, observing that, under the golden principles of criminal jurisprudence, no piece of evidence should be considered in isolation; rather, all evidence should be evaluated cumulatively alongside other available evidence. What does assuming the chairmanship of the IMF, World Bank governing boards mean for Pakistan?

Pakistan has been selected to chair the Boards of Governors of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank Group (WBG) in 2027, marking its return to the position after 74 years. The chairmanship carries mainly procedural and diplomatic responsibilities and does not give Pakistan additional authority over lending decisions by either institution. Minister for Economic Affairs Ahad Khan Cheema was announced as incoming chair during the annual meetings in Bangkok on Oct 16. Pakistan last held the position in 1953, when the finance and economic affairs minister, Mohamad Ali, chaired the eighth annual meetings of the IMF and International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) in Washington. Pakistan is not the only South Asian country to have held the chairmanship. India chaired the annual meetings in Paris in 1950, with its finance minister, Chintaman D. Deshmukh, presiding over the proceedings. Pakistan followed in 1953, Sri Lanka — then known as Ceylon — in 1968 and Nepal in 1999. How is the chair selected? The bylaws of the IMF and World Bank set out the procedure for selecting a governor to chair their annual meetings. Section 6(a) of the World Bank’s bylaws states: “At each annual meeting the Board of Governors shall select a Governor to act as Chair”. The appointment continues until the conclusion of the following annual meeting. Similarly, Section 7 of the IMF’s bylaws provides for the selection of a chair and at least two vice chairs at each regular meeting, with their terms continuing until the next regular meeting. These provisions establish the selection procedure and duration of the chairmanship, which rotates among member countries, including developing economies, rather than being reserved for major shareholders. Who is eligible? Member countries appoint governors to represent them on the Boards of Governors of the IMF and World Bank. These are generally finance ministers, central bank governors or senior government officials holding equivalent positions. However, the chairmanship is not legally restricted to finance ministers. Any governor appointed by a member country is eligible for selection, regardless of the official position they hold. Under Section 6(a) of the World Bank’s bylaws and Section 7 of the IMF’s bylaws, the respective Boards select their chairs from among these governors. Article V, Section 2(a), of the IBRD Articles of Agreement also provides for the selection of one of its governors as chairman. In Pakistan, Cheema serves as the country’s governor to the World Bank. Under the Rules of Business, the Economic Affairs Division handles matters relating to the World Bank and other multilateral development institutions, while the Finance Division deals with the IMF. Historically, the finance minister also held the economic affairs portfolio. However, the two portfolios are currently held by separate ministers, with responsibilities for the World Bank and IMF divided between their respective divisions. Cheema’s appointment as Pakistan’s governor to the World Bank made him eligible for selection as chair. Pakistan is represented on the IMF Board of Governors by the governor of the State Bank of Pakistan. Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb’s absence from the two Boards of Governors, however, does not diminish his role in Pakistan’s engagement with these institutions. Along with the central bank governor, he continues to lead the country’s economic and financial discussions during the annual meetings. What are the chair’s powers and responsibilities? Under Section 5(d) of the World Bank’s bylaws, the chair, jointly with the bank’s president, is responsible for arrangements concerning meetings of the Board of Governors. The official Rules of Conduct for Annual Meetings, approved in March 2023, further state: “The Chair of the Boards of Governors will establish the order of speaking at each session.” The chair may also communicate information about the proceedings to the press alongside the World Bank president and IMF managing director. However, the chairmanship does not confer executive authority over either institution. Although the Boards of Governors are their highest governing bodies, most operational responsibilities are delegated to their respective Executive Boards. The position does not give Pakistan additional voting rights, preferential access to financing or authority to approve IMF programmes and World Bank loans. For Pakistan, the significance of the chairmanship lies mainly in greater international representation and opportunities to engage with senior economic policymakers. It will also provide a platform to contribute to discussions on global economic and development issues, particularly the financing needs of developing countries and the mobilisation of private capital. More than 130 million voters were deleted during the revision but the Election Commission has denied favouring any party and said wrongly deleted voters can re-enrol. The BJP has accused rivals of trying to undermine democracy by wrongly questioning legitimate electoral processes. Delhi’s municipal authorities said they were closing the Central Park for two days for “administrative” reasons. Violation of the orders would “attract suitable legal action”, police said Thursday, but added that they would allow “selected persons” to gather at a “suitable place”. In recent days, the Indian capital has seen street protests over a large-scale voter roll revision exercise overseen by chief election commissioner Kumar. Critics say the removals have disproportionately affected minorities, particularly Muslims. Election authorities say the move was intended to eliminate duplicate, deceased and fraudulent entries. Members of Modi’s Hindu nationalist BJP have long alleged that large numbers of undocumented Muslim migrants from neighbouring Bangladesh have fraudulently entered India’s electoral rolls. On Friday, Defence Minister Rajnath Singh dismissed the allegations, saying the sole agenda of the opposition parties was “to destroy democratic institutions and create unrest in the country”. “Their fight is not for electoral reform… not for Gyanesh Kumar, not for the bright future of the country’s youth,” he said at a news conference. “Rather, this is their conspiracy to seize power by creating anarchy in the country.”

KP Governor Kundi announces amended welfare scheme for police officers, martyrs’ families

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi on Friday announced changes to the compensation packages for police officers martyred or injured in the line of duty as part of efforts to promote peace in the province. The provincial government has increased compensation for the families of martyred police officers and amended the previous plot allotment scheme, opting to pay the families cash in lieu of plots. Under the new scheme, the families of martyred constables, head constables, assistant sub-inspectors (ASIs), sub-inspectors (SIs) and inspectors will receive Rs17 million in compensation, up from Rs11 million. For the families of martyred deputy superintendents of police (DSPs) and assistant superintendents of police (ASPs), compensation will increase from Rs16 million to Rs25 million. The families of martyred superintendents of police (SP), senior superintendents of police (SSP), assistant inspector general (AIG) and all grade 18 and 19 officers previously received Rs21m in compensation, but under the new scheme, they will receive Rs30m. For families of martyred deputy inspector generals (DIG), inspector generals of police (IGP) and grade 20 officers, the compensation would increase from Rs21m to Rs35m. Regarding compensation for injured police officers, Kundi said an increase should have been made earlier, but it had not been. To address this, he said, the government had made “significant increases” in compensation for officers who had been severely injured or permanently disabled in the line of duty. Previously, compensation for permanent disability stood at Rs500,000. His government had increased it tenfold to Rs5 million. Compensation for severe injuries had also been increased from Rs100,000 to Rs1.5m. Kundi added that the families of martyred police officers had previously been allotted plots. However, the scheme had proved inconvenient for some families, as those living in Dera Ismail Khan or Bannu might be allotted plots in Nowshera or Swabi. Under the new scheme, the families would receive financial compensation in lieu of plots. He stressed that cash payments would give the families the freedom to decide how to use the money, whether to rent a house or buy a plot or a house in a location of their choice. For the families of martyred constables and head constables, the government will provide Rs18 million in cash in lieu of a five-marla plot. The families of martyred ASIs, SIs and inspectors will receive Rs25m in cash instead of a seven-marla plot. Kundi further said that the families of DSPs and ASPs, who were previously entitled to a 10-marla plot, would now receive Rs30m in cash. For the families of officers of SP or SSP rank and above, compensation had been set at Rs70m in cash in lieu of a one-kanal plot. Kundi stressed that not only serving police officers but also their families were at risk. Noting that terrorists could target officers’ families, he said the government had decided to introduce a new compensation package for such incidents. If a police officer’s spouse or children were killed, the family would receive Rs5m in compensation. If an officer’s parents or siblings were killed, the government would pay Rs3m in compensation. Kundi also announced a compensation package for police officers who were killed while off duty. The families of constables, head constables, ASIs and inspectors killed while off duty would receive Rs4m. The compensation for the families of DSPs and ASPs killed while off duty had been set at Rs5m. For the families of SPs, SSPs and officers in grades 18 and 19, compensation had been set at Rs7m. The KP government has also announced Rs1m in compensation to help fund the weddings of martyred officers’ daughters, describing it as “essential financial assistance at an important stage of the families’ lives”, Kundi said. Children of martyred officers will also benefit from a new fund being established to support their education, including studies abroad, Kundi said. The governor added that salaries of KP police officers had been increased by more than expected in an effort to make their pay packages more attractive than those offered in other provinces. He added that he had directed the relevant officials to expedite recruitment to the KP police, which has 17,000 vacancies. Concluding his remarks, Kundi said: “The days of rifts between the Centre and the province are gone. We now have to work together for the province’s peace and prosperity.” Kundi had on Tuesday directed the authorities to prepare and submit, at the earliest, a summary containing proposals for an increase in police salaries and an enhanced welfare package for the families of police martyrs. Chairing a high-level meeting at the Governor House, he reviewed proposals relating to police salaries and welfare measures for the families of martyred and injured police personnel. On Oct 11, the federal government imposed emergency and governor’s rule in KP, under Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution, making Kundi the leader of the province.

18 countries, including Pakistan, condemn inauguration of Colombian embassy in Jerusalem

Eighteen countries, including Pakistan, on Friday strongly condemned the opening of the Colombian embassy in Jerusalem, stating that any move to recognise the city as Israel’s capital had no legal effect. Colombia on Wednesday officially opened its embassy in Jerusalem, joining a small group of countries including the United States in taking the symbolic step of backing Israeli sovereignty in the contested city. The vast majority of countries keep their embassies to Israel in Tel Aviv on the grounds that the status of Jerusalem will be settled in a final resolution of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. In a joint statement shared by the Foreign Office (FO), the foreign ministers of Pakistan, Algeria, Djibouti, Egypt, Indonesia, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Malaysia, Oman, Palestine, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, Syria, Sudan and Turkiye said they “strongly condemn and express their unequivocal rejection of the inauguration by the Republic of Colombia of its embassy in Jerusalem”. The statement said the development was in contravention of relevant United Nations Security Council (UNSC) resolutions and undermined international efforts to achieve a “just and lasting peace based on the two-state solution”. “The foreign ministers reaffirmed that any measure or decision intended to alter the legal or historical status of occupied Jerusalem, or to recognise it as Israel’s capital, is null and void and has no legal effect,” the joint statement said. “This position is consistent with relevant UNSC and General Assembly resolutions, particularly Security Council resolutions 476 (1980) and 478 (1980), which reject attempts to change Jerusalem’s character or status and call on states to withdraw diplomatic missions from the city, which is part of the Palestinian territory occupied in 1967,” it added. As per the joint statement, the foreign ministers called upon the Colombian government to “reconsider and reverse this decision, uphold its obligations under international law, and refrain from measures that prejudice the legitimate rights of the Palestinian people, including their right to an independent and sovereign Palestinian State with East Jerusalem as its capital”. In May this year, Pakistan, along with 13 other countries, also condemned the “illegal and unacceptable step” of the so-called Somaliland opening a “purported” embassy in occupied Jerusalem, terming it an “infringement on the legal and historical status of Jerusalem”. US President Donald Trump, during his first term, moved the US embassy to Jerusalem and recognised the city as Israel’s capital. Since Trump’s move, only a handful of small, US-aligned countries have followed suit — Fiji, Guatemala, Honduras, Papua New Guinea, Paraguay and Nauru.
