IHC chief justice seeks copy of inquiry report on fire at Pims, details of action taken

ISLAMABAD: The Islamabad High Court (IHC) has sought the report submitted to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on the fire at the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences (Pims) and details of the action taken, while also questioning the culpability of the hospital’s executive director (ED). Chief Justice Sardar Muhammad Sarfaraz Dogar passed the directions while hearing two separate petitions related to the Pims tragedy. During the hearing of a petition filed by lawyer Qasim Iqbal Jalali seeking a judicial inquiry into the incident, Additional Attorney General (AAG) Rashid Hafeez, Deputy Attorney General Faisal Irfan and Assistant Attorney General Azmat Bashir Tarar appeared before the court. Justice Dogar termed the Pims fire a “terrible incident” and observed that the loss of 14 children was extremely serious. The petitioner’s counsel argued that the government had constituted committees comprising bureaucrats, questioning how the officials of one department could conduct an inquiry into their own colleagues. “How can bureaucrats inquire into their own department?” the counsel asked. The chief justice questioned the legal basis of the request for a judicial inquiry, asking the lawyer under which law the court could order such an inquiry and where the Inquiry Act empowered the IHC to issue such directives. The counsel referred to the commission constituted after the Punjab Institute of Cardiology incident. The chief justice observed that the said commission had not been constituted by the court, adding that such matters fell within the government’s domain. “If there is any matter with us, I will ask one of my judges to look into it,” Justice Dogar remarked. The counsel maintained that “no one can be the judge of his own actions” and argued that responsibility had not been fixed in the inquiry report submitted so far. AAG Hafeez informed the court that the health secretary had been suspended on the day of the incident, while hospital officials had subsequently been suspended on the basis of an interim inquiry report. Justice Dogar again termed the incident extremely tragic and asked whether any action was still being taken and whether there was any restriction preventing the court from passing an order. The court sought the report submitted to the prime minister, along with details of the action taken so far and directed the federation to submit a detailed response. A separate petition also sought removal of the Pims ED from office — who has already been suspended — and action over the tragedy. The petitioner’s counsel highlighted the conditions at the hospital and questioned the utilisation of its massive budget. The counsel told the court that the deaths of the children were horrific and requested that the Pims ED be removed from his post. “How can the court give a direction to remove him from the post?” Chief Justice Dogar asked. The counsel contended that Pims has an annual budget of around Rs20 to Rs22 billion, yet several children were being accommodated on a single bed. He questioned where the budget was being spent and urged the court to order the executive director’s removal and return his services to his parent department. The chief justice observed that he believed the Pims ED had already been removed from the post, referring to the prime minister’s orders to suspend Prof Dr Imran Sikandar and seven others. National Institutes of Health (NIH) Chief Executive Officer Dr Mohammad Salman has taken charge of the post after being appointed as the acting Pims ED. The counsel replied that although the ED had been removed, no responsibility had been fixed against him and he had not been held responsible in the inquiry report either. AAG Hafeez told the court that the report currently available was only an interim report and that the complete inquiry report would be submitted within five days. The chief justice specifically asked whether responsibility had so far been fixed against the Pims ED. The court sought a report on the matter and adjourned the hearing. CDA response sought over alleged lack of safety NOCs Separately, the IHC has sought a response from the federal capital’s administration over the alleged non-implementation of fire safety laws and regulations in Islamabad. Justice Dogar heard the petition filed by citizen Malik Naveed Ashiq Awan through his counsel, Advocate Afrasiab Ahmed Rana. The petition was filed in the wake of the Pims incident. The petitioner contended that even a major healthcare facility such as Pims did not have the requisite fire department no-objection certificate (NOC). The petition further alleged that commercial, educational and medical buildings across Islamabad were operating without fire safety NOCs and that several buildings lacked essential fire-fighting equipment, emergency exits and fire alarms. The petitioner pleaded that the Islamabad Fire Prevention and Life Safety Regulations 2010 should be implemented in letter and spirit. The petition sought directions for an immediate fire safety audit of all buildings in the federal capital and the sealing of buildings operating without valid fire safety NOCs. It also sought the establishment of a regular inspection mechanism to identify and monitor buildings that are illegal or pose a threat to public safety. The court issued notices to the home secretary, Metropolitan Corporation Islamabad (MCI), and the offices of the Capital Development Authority (CDA) chairman and Islamabad chief commissioner — both roles served by retired Lt Sohail Ashraf — seeking their responses. The petition maintained that the Pims fire had exposed serious shortcomings in the enforcement of fire safety regulations and called for immediate institutional action to prevent similar incidents in the future. Following the Pims incident, CDA intensified enforcement of fire and life safety regulations across Islamabad, sealing several high-rise buildings and partially sealing two hotels in Blue Area. It also imposed a fine of Rs500,000 each on Pims, Polyclinic and its own Capital Hospital for “non-compliance with Fire and Life Safety Requirements”. Final inquiry report awaited Meanwhile, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry said that the inquiry committee’s investigation into the Pims incident had not been completed yet. Speaking to the media in Islamabad, he said the inquiry was ongoing, not just into the gynaecology department but into all departments of the hospital. “More names can come up in the final report. Anyone found guilty of negligence will be punished,” Chaudhry said. Acknowledging that “the innocent lives lost cannot be brought back”, the minister stressed that action against those responsible was essential to avoid similar incidents in the future. Speaking on behalf of PM Shehbaz, Chaudhry vowed that zero tolerance would be shown towards those found guilty of negligence. Safety arrangements and precautions at Pims were being closely monitored by the health ministry, he told the media.

IHC seeks replies from ministries, Pemra, PTA on plea seeking to regulate children's social media use

ISLAMABAD: The Islamabad High Court (IHC) on Monday sought responses from relevant ministries and authorities on a petition seeking a framework to regulate social media use by children under 16 years of age. The plea, filed by petitioner Waqas Nasir through advocates Muhammad Jalal Haider and Yahya Farid Khawaja earlier this month, seeks legislation on the matter and the introduction of an effective age-verification mechanism for social media accounts. It was taken up by IHC Chief Justice Sardar Muhammad Sarfaraz Dogar, who issued notices to the Ministry of Information Technology and Telecommunication, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, Ministry of Interior, Ministry of Law and Justice, Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) and Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority (Pemra) and directed them to submit their replies within 15 days. During the hearing, Advocates Haider and Khawaja appeared before the court. Advocate Haider argued that an effective age-verification mechanism should be introduced for social media accounts operated by children. He contended that legislation was urgently required to protect children from cyberbullying, online harassment, harmful and inappropriate content and other risks associated with the use of social media platforms. The lawyer argued that safeguarding the best interests of children was a “constitutional and legal responsibility of the state” and that the government should introduce effective legislation in line with international practices. When Justice Dogar enquired whether Pemra had any law dealing with the issue, Advocate Haider said Pemra had legal powers to take action against a person on the basis of a complaint, but currently, there was no specific legislation regulating children’s access to social media. The IHC CJ further asked whether any law had so far been enacted specifically for this purpose, to which the lawyer replied in the negative. Subsequently, Justice Dogar issued notices to the relevant ministries and authorities, seeking their responses within 15 days. The court also directed the law officer to ensure implementation of the notices. The plea In his petition, Nasir urged the court to direct the government to formulate a comprehensive legal and regulatory framework for the online protection of children, including special safeguards governing their access to social media. The plea contended that children were increasingly exposed to the harmful effects of social media, including cyberbullying, online harassment, and inappropriate or harmful content, requiring the state to introduce a comprehensive legal and regulatory mechanism for their protection. The petitioner urged the court to direct the government to formulate a policy covering digital rights, privacy, and mental and physical safety of children, and to impose specific responsibilities on social media platforms to safeguard minors online. The plea stated that several countries around the world had already introduced age restrictions and safety measures for children’s use of social media and contended that legislation in this connection would be part of an emerging international trend for protecting children in the digital environment. It relied on the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child and General Comment No. 25, arguing that protecting the best interests of children was not merely a policy objective but a legal obligation of the state. The matter had also come into the limelight in the Punjab Assembly last month, when a resolution was submitted for a nationwide ban on social media accounts for children under the age of 16 without parental consent. The resolution, moved by MPA Sarah Ahmad, urged the provincial government to recommend to the federal government to introduce comprehensive legislation to regulate children’s access to social media platforms and strengthen online safety measures.

Studying the invisible With a field of view more than 100 times larger than Hubble’s — and significantly wider than James Webb’s — Roman will survey vast swaths of the sky from a vantage point 1.5 million kilometres (930,000 miles) from Earth, which it will take about 100 days to reach. Designed to work in tandem with other observatories such as Webb, which offers greater power and precision, Roman aims to discover thousands of exoplanets as well as tens of thousands of supernovae, the explosive deaths of massive stars. “It will be the biggest catalog of astronomical objects anybody’s ever produced, and it will help us understand how common are solar systems like our own,” Dave Content, Roman’s technical manager, told AFP. The new telescope will also shed light on two of the biggest mysteries in physics: dark matter and dark energy. Their origins remain unknown, but together they are believed to make up about 95 per cent of the universe. Dark matter is thought to act as a kind of gravitational glue, while dark energy is believed to be a repulsive force driving the universe’s expansion.
