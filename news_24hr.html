OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the company had disclosed the May 13 event, privately notified Hugging Face about the activity flagged by Wiedermann-Moeller and was “committed to transparency about these issues and to sharing what we learn as our review continues.” Hugging Face, recently acquired by chipmaker Nvidia, did not respond to requests for comment. What's behind the foul smell in Karachi today?

Residents of Karachi, particularly those residing near the sea, woke up on Wednesday morning to a foul smell which some described as “fishy”. According to experts, decaying microscopic marine algae and current weather conditions are behind the pungent smell permeating the air. Muhammad Moazzam Khan, technical adviser on marine resources at World Wide Fund for Nature-Pakistan (WWF-P), said the fishy smell was a result of decomposing microscopic marine algae called phytoplankton — a naturally occurring event. He explained that when the regional monsoon system ends in mid-September, it results in significant changes in the sea conditions. Consequently, these phytoplankton, found abundantly during the monsoon, begin to decay. Phytoplankton are found in the upper 100 metres of the sea, where sunlight can penetrate, and are not visible to the naked eye. The WWF-P expert said this phenomenon — also witnessed in 2024 — often causes a foul smell to permeate the air in the coastal areas. He added that the smell would likely dissipate in a few days. The Pakistan Meteorological Department’s (PMD) spokesperson, Anjum Nazir Zaigham, and climate change expert Fatima Yamin attributed the scale of the smell to the weather conditions too. Yamin said a low-pressure area off the coast of India’s Jamnagar was circulating wind around Karachi’s coastline, which was turning into smog because of air pollution. She added that the combination of smog and the weather conditions was causing the smell to “circulate throughout the city”.
