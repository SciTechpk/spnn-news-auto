Cabinet approves deregulation of sugar sector to meet vital IMF benchmark

ISLAMABAD: To meet a long-postponed structural benchmark of the International Monetary Fund (IMF), the federal cabinet on Tuesday approved the deregulation of the sugar sector, an end to its export subsidies and free movement among the provinces. The cabinet approved the ‘National Policy for Deregulation of Sugar Sector’ through circulation while Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif “recused himself from the sugar sector related decisions” owing to his immediate family’s sugar business, informed sources told Dawn. The decision came after the government allowed the export of more than 400,000 tonnes of sugar on Friday, in addition to 200,000 tonnes already exported a few months ago. The IMF has continuously pushed for a policy ending the government’s role in sugar sector operations, from sugarcane crop prices to sugar production and import-export, leaving it to market forces. The fund also minutely remained engaged with the terms and conditions of a ministerial committee which finalised the sugar deregulation policy. Pakistan has been in breach of the structural benchmark of the IMF since June 2026 and its approval was mandatory for scheduling the IMF executive board meeting, now set for Dec 11. The policy approval was hampered by Sindh’s virtual veto, although three other provinces had already consented to the draft. After hectic backdoor negotiations, the Sindh government finally cleared the policy on Nov 21 after securing certain amendments. The policy was finalised by a ministerial committee led by Power Minister Awais Leghari. The committee also includes the ministers for finance, food security and economic affairs; the premier’s adviser for industries; the chairman of the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR); secretaries of concerned ministries and provincial chief secretaries, as well as other stakeholders. Being a structural benchmark of the IMF, the timeline for the completion of the process was June 30, 2026. The fund proposed revised Terms of Reference (ToRs) on July 31, 2026 for incorporation in the ToRs of the committee, which were agreed to by the prime minister, official records seen by Dawn suggested. The Ministry of Industries and Production, on the IMF’s directions, “inserted some amendments and shared the said draft policy with the IMF” by the end of August, which were fine-tuned by the fund again on Oct 1. It was insisted that the Sindh government’s agreement was necessary for completion of the structural benchmark. The policy provides a predictable mechanism for imports and exports with a complete end to export subsidies, the cabinet was told. There will be no restrictions on the inter-provincial movement of sugar, and transitional arrangements have been provided for the current crushing season. At the input stage, the government has removed restrictions on planting zones and cane varieties, providing freedom to cane producers to sell their crop to any buyer and mills to sell their product. The sugarcane price has now been left to market forces to determine, but there will be a negative list of banned sugar varieties. At the processing stage, there will be no restriction on the establishment of new mills or capacity approvals for mills; the millers will have the freedom to choose raw material sources and take market-based production decisions. Price controls by the governments will come to an end. For creating an enabling environment for smooth transition, the deregulation policy provides certain safeguards to the farmers in the shape of third-party validation of weighing scales, sucrose testing framework and interest payments by millers on the late payment of sugarcane dues. The sugarcane cess has been promised for effective use of research, as well as easy access to credit for farmers and millers, along with farm mechanisation schemes going ahead. The policy, nevertheless, retains the government’s right to maintain buffer sugar stocks and take measures to access correct and timely information on sugarcane crop, sugar production and market availability. It will also encourage working towards mandating ethanol blending and providing a fair and competitive market.
