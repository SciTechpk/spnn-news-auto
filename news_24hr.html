Constitutional provisions Emergency Article 232 of the Constitution provides for the proclamation of emergency in a province. It says, “If the president is satisfied that a grave emergency exists in which the security of Pakistan, or any part thereof, is threatened by war or external aggression, or by internal disturbance beyond the power of a provincial government to control, he may issue a Proclamation of Emergency.” However, invoking this article “due to internal disturbances beyond the powers of a provincial government to control” requires a resolution from the provincial assembly of that province. In case the president “acts on his own” — which is not currently the case — the Proclamation of Emergency is to be placed before both houses of Parliament for approval by each house within 10 days. According to this provision, parliament “shall have power to make laws for a province, or any part thereof, with respect to any matter not enumerated in the Federal Legislative List” during the time of the proclamation of an emergency. Nothing in this Article shall restrict the power of a provincial assembly to make any law which the Constitution empowers it to make, but if any provision of a provincial law is repugnant to any provision of an Act by Parliament, the latter would prevail and the provincial law would be void as long as the parliamentary Act is in effect. Governor’s rule On the other hand, Article 234 deals with the imposition of the governor’s rule “in case of failure of constitutional machinery in a province”. For imposition of the governor’s rule, the president simply requires a request from the provincial governor, it says. “A proclamation issued under this Article shall be laid before a joint sitting and shall cease to be in force at the expiration of two months, unless before the expiration of that period it has been approved by resolution of the joint sitting and may by like resolution be extended for a further period not exceeding two months at a time,” the Constitution reads. However, it adds that no such proclamation shall in any case remain in force for more than six months. Article 233, which provides powers to the president to suspend certain fundamental rights during the emergency period, was not mentioned in the summary signed by President Zardari.
