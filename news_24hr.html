Pemra bans ads, promotions for commercial infant formula on electronic media

ISLAMABAD: The Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority (Pemra) on Monday banned advertisements and promotional activities for commercially manufactured infant formula milk on electronic media with immediate effect, directing television channels and other regulated broadcasters to stop airing such advertisements. The ban will remain in force until the required legal compliance and no-objection certificate, approval or permission is obtained from the concerned department or health regulatory body. According to an order issued by Pemra, the decision was taken following a letter from the Ministry of National Health Services, Regulations and Coordination dated Nov 18, regarding implementation of the Protection of Breastfeeding and Child Nutrition Ordinance 2002. The order said that Pemra’s committee deliberated on sections 7, 8 and 9 of the ordinance, which prohibit certain advertisements, promotional activities, distribution of free samples and direct marketing of designated products. The committee also reviewed relevant provisions of the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan Act 2012, the Pemra Ordinance 2002 and the Pemra Electronic Media (Programmes and Advertisements) Code of Conduct 2015. Invoking Section 27 of the Pemra Ordinance 2002, the authority declared that “advertisement(s) of infant formula products (commercially manufactured formula powder milk) shall be prohibited forthwith”. The authority has also referred the matter to its Council of Complaints for recommendations in accordance with the provisions of the Pemra Ordinance. Pakistan is among the 118 countries that voted in favour of adopting the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes during the World Health Assembly in May 1981. It enacted a national breastfeeding law in October 2002 in the shape of a presidential ordinance to enforce the code, which also envisaged a National Infant Feeding Board. The Senate Standing Committee on National Health Services called on Federal Health Minister Mustafa Kamal earlier this month to enforce the ordinance, warning that weak implementation undermines child health. The committee chairman, Senator Amir Waliuddin Chishti, expressed concern over the law’s lack of enforcement despite being in place for over 20 years. The Sindh government in August launched World Breastfeeding Week with a renewed commitment to strictly enforce the province’s law against the promotion of infant formula. Sindh Health and Population Welfare Minister Dr Azra Fazal Pechuho said breastfeeding remained one of the most effective interventions for protecting newborns, improving child nutrition and reducing infant mortality.

Important precedent Israeli legal scholar Yuval Shany said the ruling could broaden the arguments available to South Africa in its case against Israel.
