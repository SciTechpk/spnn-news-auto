LHC issues judgement in 8-year-long public litigation, reaffirms environmental directions

LAHORE: The Lahore High Court (LHC) on Thursday issued its final judgment in an eight-year-old public interest litigation concerning smog and environmental degradation in Punjab, reaffirming its directions and achievements during the marathon proceedings. Justice Shahid Karim, who authored the judgment, described it as the “last judgment in the trilogy”, following two earlier reported rulings in 2023 and 2025 arising from the same continuing proceedings. Justice Karim, who is due to reach superannuation on August 19, began his judgment with a line from the final song in the play Hamilton: “Legacy, what is a legacy? It’s planting seeds in a garden you never get to see. I wrote some notes at the beginning of a song someone will sing for me.” The judge noted that the judgment culminated the extraordinary and seminal work accomplished in a novel and paradigmatic manner through the judicial review of public actions. “It does not mean that the goal has been accomplished. Far from it. Yet it sets the tone for future actions and lays the groundwork for building resilience and adaptation policies,” he added. He said that, during the course of the proceedings, governments had come on board, though reluctantly at times, and pursued policies based on the court’s decisions. He observed: “A robust culture of decision-making has developed with environmental and water conservation at its heart.” Justice Karim held that there had been critical questioning of the court’s authority to continue the proceedings unabated for more than eight years. “The short answer is the highly consequential issues animating and forming the core of this litigation,” he explained. Key directions and their compliance reaffirmed and consolidated in the judgment included: Smog as a calamity: On the court’s directions, smog was formally declared a calamity, with the Punjab Disaster Management Authority (PDMA) tasked with leading a dedicated control room for enforcement during the smog season. Brick kilns: Every operational brick kiln in Punjab has been converted to Zig-Zag Technology to cut emissions and each kiln has been assigned a Unique Identification Number for digital monitoring by the Environment Protection Agency. Stubble burning: The court raised penalties for stubble burning violations to Rs200,000 per acre after finding some districts were imposing fines as low as Rs50,000, and directed uniform enforcement across all Deputy Commissioners’ offices. School transport: Private schools have been directed to arrange buses or vans for at least 50 per cent of their students as a condition for registration renewal, to be implemented in phases. Water conservation: Amendments were made to LDA Building and Zoning Regulations making rainwater harvesting mandatory for new construction, and Gray Water Treatment Plants compulsory for all new commercial and residential building plans, with no building approvals to be granted without compliance. As many as 563 service stations across Lahore now operate water recycling plants, cutting fresh water use per car wash by roughly 70pc (from 400 litres to about 120 litres). Ablution water recycling tanks have been installed at over 194 mosques, including a 60,000–70,000-gallon facility at Data Darbar. Groundwater conservation: In compliance with the court’s orders, Wasa Lahore has collected over Rs3.35 billion in aquifer charges from bulk groundwater users since 2018. Separately, 117 recharge wells have been installed across Lahore, while rainwater storage tanks — including a 4-million-gallon facility at Gaddafi Stadium — have been built to combat flooding and recharge aquifers. Protection of public green spaces: The court barred playgrounds and open spaces — including at institutions such as MAO College — from being used for weddings or commercial events, and directed authorities, including DHA, LDA, and the School Education Department to protect and maintain green cover. Judicial review of development projects: The judgment also references two related rulings — the 2023 quashing of the Lahore Division Master Plan 2050 for violating LDA Master Plan Rules, and the striking down of land acquisition for a Gujrat Divisional Complex that would have required felling over 594 kanals of forest land — both cited as major environmental victories achieved through the same broader litigation. In his concluding remarks, Justice Karim paid tribute to the Judicial Water and Environment Commission’s members — retired Justice Ali Akbar Qureshi, Syed Kamal Ali Haider, Hina Hafeezullah Ishaq, and Ch. Zulfiqar Ali — who served pro bono throughout the proceedings. The judge recommended that they and several government officers involved in the implementation of the court’s directions be considered for civil awards. The judge disposed of the main petition filed by Haroon Farooq in 2018, along with a dozen petitions subsequently filed before the court concerning water and environmental issues. Justice Karim concluded the judgment with a poem by Amanda Gorman: “Let the globe, if nothing else, say this is time, that even as we grieved, we grew, that even as we hurt, we hoped, that even as we tired, we tried.”

NCCIA arrests 114 foreigners over alleged cyber-romance, sextortion network

ISLAMABAD: The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) has arrested 114 foreign nationals, including 111 Chinese and three Vietnamese citizens, for allegedly running a cyber-romance and sextortion network from a registered IT business in the capital. According to details released by the NCCIA’s media wing, the accused allegedly used electronic means and online platforms to target individuals in different countries. The network was allegedly involved in luring foreign nationals into online sexual conversations, including video chats and explicit video calls, and subsequently exploiting the material for criminal and financial purposes. Officials said the investigation also revealed indicators linking the accused to organised cybercrime activities, including cyber-romance-related financial fraud. NCCIA Islamabad has registered the case under Sections 14 and 21 of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (Peca) 2016, read with Sections 419, 420, 34 and 109 of the Pakistan Penal Code (PPC). Section 14 of Peca deals with electronic fraud. It criminalises using any information system, device or data to deceive a person for wrongful gain. It covers phishing, online scams and running fraudulent setups online. Offences under the section are punishable with up to two years’ imprisonment, a fine of up to Rs10 million, or both. Section 21 deals with offences against the modesty of a natural person and a minor. It covers sexual blackmail, revenge porn, deepfakes and inducing a person to engage in sexually explicit acts online to cause harm, blackmail or extort them. It also provides for the immediate removal or blocking of such content by the NCCIA upon a complaint. Offences under the section are punishable with up to five years’ imprisonment and a fine of up to Rs5 million. The punishment is more severe if the victim is a minor. Section 419 of the PPC deals with cheating by personation. It prescribes a punishment of up to seven years’ imprisonment, a fine, or both for impersonating another person to cheat. It also covers the use of fake profiles and identities on social media platforms. Section 420 of the PPC applies when a person is deceived into paying money or handing over property. It covers the financial fraud involved in cyber-romance scams, with the offence punishable by up to seven years’ imprisonment and a fine. Sections 34 and 109 of the PPC deal with common intention and abetment, respectively, and have been invoked to hold all members of the organised network liable, including planners, facilitators and financiers. The agency said further investigation was underway to establish the complete role of all those involved, identify additional victims and beneficiaries, and trace the financial and digital infrastructure used in the alleged activities. The NCCIA has also shared a list of the arrested individuals, along with their passport numbers.

Barrister Gilani further submitted that the petitioner had approached the office of the Interior Secretary several times seeking issuance of the renewed passport, but received no positive response from the authorities. He said the petitioner subsequently served a legal notice on the Interior Secretary, but no response was received. The counsel argued that the continued refusal to issue the passport was illegal and amounted to a violation of the petitioner’s fundamental rights.
