FCC bars PHC from hearing cases related to PTI workers' preventive detention

ISLAMABAD: The Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) on Thursday restrained the Peshawar High Court (PHC) from hearing cases related to the preventive detention of PTI workers and activists under the Maintenance of Public Order Ordinance (MPO) following the imposition of emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The directive was issued by a two-judge bench, headed by Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan and including Justice Aamer Farooq. “Since the armed forces have been called to act in aid of civil power in the entire province of KP pursuant to the Oct 11 order … the proceedings before the PHC instituted under Article 199 on or after Oct 12 shall, in terms of clause 4 of Article 245, remain suspended,” ordered the two-judge bench. The bench took up a set of petitions challenging interim bail of the detainees, granted by the PHC against surety bonds of Rs100,000 each. The PHC had issued the interim orders in the absence of any report by the provincial government explaining reasons and circumstances that led to the detention of PTI activists and also directed that they should not be arrested pending the cases related to their detentions before the high court. The PHC will next hear the case on Nov 12. The court order also directed KP Advocate General Sanaullah Khan to furnish a report to the top court within seven days in relation to the areas in KP where the armed forces were no longer required to act in aid of civil power. The FCC said it may consider modifying the present order in terms of clause 4 of Article 245, which provides for calling in armed forces in aid of civil forces, after examining the report so furnished. The FCC also issued notice to the respondents, saying since important questions involving interpretation of the Constitution were involved, notice should also be issued to the attorney general for Pakistan under Order XXVIIA of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. More than 60 appeals challenging the PHC’s interim orders have been fixed before the FCC. The pleas were moved by the KP Home and Tribal Affairs Department additional chief secretary. In its order, the FCC observed that the points raised during the hearing needed serious consideration, recalling that the earlier judgment of the PHC in Aftab Alam’s case, relying on which the impugned orders have been passed, required careful examination to ascertain whether the view adopted by the high court on Article 245 of the Constitution was constitutionally correct. For the purposes of the instant petitions, FCC also referred to clauses 3 and 4 of Article 245 of the Constitution in its order. Under clause 3, a high court shall not exercise any jurisdiction under Article 199 in relation to any area in which the armed forces of Pakistan are, for the time being, acting in aid of civil power in pursuance of Article 245: Provided that this clause shall not be deemed to affect the jurisdiction of the High Court in respect of any proceeding pending immediately before the day on which the Armed Forces start acting in aid of civil power. Likewise, under clause 4, any proceeding in relation to an area referred to in clause 3 instituted on or after the day the armed forces start acting in aid of civil power and pending in any high court shall remain suspended for the period during which the armed forces are so acting. Prima facie, clause 3 of Article 245 is not an ouster clause in the nature as it appears elsewhere in the Constitution such as Article 236(2), where despite an ouster, the courts have examined the constitutionality of any proclamation issued under Part X of the Constitution, the FCC observed. Clause 3 of Article 245 referred to a particular situation which is emergent but temporary in nature, the order said. Thus, the bar on exercising jurisdiction also lasts for so long as the armed forces are acting in aid of civil power. This and other questions raised in the instant proceedings require a careful examination and an authoritative pronouncement by the FCC, the order emphasised. The FCC order also recalled how the proclamation was issued by the President on Oct 11, 2026 under Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution, following which the federal government assumed control of KP through the governor. Following the proclamation, the federal government authorised deployment of Pakistan Army in aid of civil power for safety of life, security of person and property of citizens as well as security of government installations in KP vide Oct 11 order issued under Article 245 of the Constitution, the order noted. Pursuant to the said orders, the Ministry of Interior requisitioned troops of armed forces vide office memorandum dated Oct 12, 2026. The Ministry of Defence made the troops deployed in the KP to act in aid of civil power across the province vide office memorandum of the even date, the order said. Consequently, several persons were placed in preventive detention under the Maintenance of Public Order Ordinance, 1960 (MPO) for maintenance of public peace and order. The detention orders issued under section 3 of the MPO were assailed before the PHC, which, while relying on its 2024 judgment titled Aftab Alam and another vs. Secretary Home and Tribal Affairs, KP government released them in limine, the order stated. The KP advocate general, who pleaded the case before the FCC on behalf of the provincial government, told Dawn that the PHC’s orders had been challenged under Article 245 of the Constitution. Lahore’s air pollution a year-round problem, worsened by winter and agricultural burning: report

LAHORE: Lahore’s fine particulate matter pollution remained above Pakistan’s prescribed standards for the past 12 months, including the relatively cleaner summer months, indicating that the city’s air pollution remained a year-round problem intensified by winter weather and seasonal agricultural burning, according to an analysis. The report, titled ‘Lahore’s smog: The year-round baseline, and what changes in October ’, was released by the environmental monitoring project ‘Hamari Hawa’ on November 2 and prepared by its founder, Hammad Tariq. It drew on ground-monitoring data, satellite observations and atmospheric reanalysis to examine pollution levels and possible sources around Lahore. On Monday, air pollution in Lahore reached hazardous levels, with the Air Quality Index (AQI) crossing 530 and fine particulate matter (PM2.5) concentrations climbing to 343 micrograms per cubic metre. According to the analysis, Lahore’s monthly average PM2.5 concentration remained above 35 micrograms per cubic metre from October 2025 to October 2026. Even in the city’s cleaner months, April to September 2026, monthly averages ranged from 40 to 59 micrograms per cubic metre, against the national annual standard of 15 micrograms per cubic metre. The report used IQAir’s historical data, which aggregates readings from ground monitors across the city. It recorded a monthly average of 40 micrograms per cubic metre in June 2026, the lowest during the period, while averages from October 2025 to February 2026 ranged between 124 and 183 micrograms per cubic metre. October 2026 recorded 100 micrograms per cubic metre. The report cautioned that the national limit of 35 micrograms per cubic metre is a 24-hour standard, whereas the monthly figures are monthly averages. The comparison therefore showed the persistence of pollution but does not establish how many individual days exceeded the daily limit. The analysis identified a sharp seasonal decline in the height of the atmospheric layer into which pollutants can disperse as one of the factors behind the winter increase. Using ERA5 atmospheric reanalysis data, it estimated that the average mixing height over Lahore fell from around 830 to 860 metres in May and June to about 400 metres in October and 260 metres in November.
