The easing was recorded in both places, in rural areas where inflation fell to 9.5pc from 10.1pc, while urban inflation edged down to 9.8pc from 10.5pc. The October reading was also within the Ministry of Finance’s projection of 9.5pc to 10.5pc for the month in its Monthly Economic Update and Outlook for October. According to the finance ministry, easing headline inflation offers some relief, while improved crop supplies can help ease food price pressures. However, despite the decline in the annual rate, prices rose 1.1pc month-on-month in October. The annual inflation rate was 6.2pc in October last year. Food inflation dropped to 7.8pc in urban areas in October from 8.1pc in September. The decline was much sharper in rural areas, where food inflation fell to 7.1pc from 7.7pc a month earlier. However, non-food inflation moved in the opposite direction, indicating continued pressure on household expenses. Urban non-food inflation decelerated to 10.6pc from 11.5pc in September, while rural non-food inflation fell to 12.3pc from 13.1pc. The latest data shows that the composition of inflation shifted in October. While food inflation slowed sharply, electricity and transport costs continued to rise. Electricity charges increased by 8.50pc from the previous month, while transport prices rose by 30.39pc in urban areas.
