The developers in China are also concerned about the future. Beijing has dismissed Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s warning that “a Chinese lead in AI would pose a grave danger for the US and the world” as “fearmongering”. China has widely deployed AI in functions ranging from mass surveillance to manufacturing, but at least Xi Jinping openly acknowledges the need for AI to “always remain under human control”. China’s state security minister, Chen Yixin, has noted the risks of “hostile forces” waging a “cognitive war” that would threaten “our political security, institutional security and ideological security”. It is perhaps somewhat ironic that China, with its open-source AI models, is more willing than the US to pursue internationally agreed constraints on the pursuit of a technology that potentially offers innumerable benefits to humankind in a wide range of spheres, but is also capable of eradicating humanity. Its deployment as a military option from Israel and the US to Russia and Ukraine is an indicator of its destructive capacity. Were AI agents to conclude that hu­­m­an beings were me­­rely standing in the way of their autono­mous­­ly conceived goals, it might seem logical to eliminate the im­­pediment. And given the extent to which we, knowi­n­g­­ly or otherwise, rely on technology for our survival, that aim wouldn’t be all that hard to accomplish. Israel has already demonstrated that, albeit with human agency at the helm. The dismissal of the documentary NAZA, which scored an unprecedented standing ovation and a Special Jury Prize in Venice last week, as a “blood libel”, is a typical Nazi-like response. What might be seen as critical mass for AI comes at a time when global politics are in disarray. Like all emerging technologies in previous centuries, the human value of AI depends on how it is deployed. It can serve as a tremendously useful tool, provided its destructive capacities are constrained. But what are the chances of that in a realm where Trump and his acolytes rule the roost? Long before the advent of the internet, there were warnings from prescient scientists. Losing all hope could be premature. But, frequently through human existence, hoping for the best and preparing for the worst has been the least disastrous option.
